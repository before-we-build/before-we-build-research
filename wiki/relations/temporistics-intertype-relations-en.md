# Proposed Temporistics Intertype Relations

This is a provisional Before We Build reconstruction, not canonical Temporistics doctrine. Contact signatures can be derived from the structure of four temporal aspects and four positions, but their interpretation requires validation.

<!-- section:1-executive-summary -->
## 1. Executive Summary

Sources support four aspects, four positions, 24 permutations, six tetrads, and special attention to the third aspect. No completed validated relation matrix comparable to the Socionics system was found.

```text
structural signature = mechanically derived from codes
relation name = BWB explanatory label
interaction claim = empirical hypothesis
```

Structural confidence is limited to formal correctness; empirical confidence is low.

<!-- section:3-core-assumptions -->
## 3. Core Assumptions

The working position model says that the first position confidently sets a temporal frame, the second develops it flexibly, the third is experienced as vulnerable, and the fourth has lower salience and more often accepts an external frame. These are hypotheses about processes, not claims about dignity or maturity.

The central question is whether a pair can build coherence around memory, present location, direction, and meaning—or whether a particular contact contributes to fragmentation.

<!-- section:4-structural-derivation-method -->
## 4. Structural Derivation Method

For `EPNF = 1E,2P,3N,4F` and `FNPE = 1F,2N,3P,4E`, the `EPNF→FNPE` signature is `4321`. The digits are the positions of A's aspects in B. This is a code for a directed pair relation, not a type of person.

<!-- section:4-1-signature-algorithm -->
### 4.1 Signature Algorithm

1. Map the position of each aspect in B.
2. Read A's aspects in positions 1–4.
3. For each, record its position in B.
4. When direction is asymmetric, show A→B and B→A separately.

<!-- section:5-proposed-relation-principles -->
## 5. Proposed Relation Principles

<!-- section:5-1-same-aspect-position-contacts -->
### 5.1 Same-Aspect Position Contacts

| Contact | Possible resource | Possible friction | What to check |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1↔1 | recognition of a confident frame | struggle over temporal authority | how a joint decision is made |
| 1↔2 | direction plus flexible development | covert hierarchy | whether roles are agreed |
| 1↔3 | clarity and acceleration | pressure, shame, defensiveness | safety and the right to stop the contact |
| 1↔4 | anchor for an unattended zone | control and dependence | whether autonomy remains |
| 2↔2 | joint processing | delay without closure | whether a decision results |
| 2↔3 | gentle help for a vulnerable zone | unwanted therapeutic framing | whether help was requested |
| 2↔4 | light guidance | topic may be unimportant to the receiver | whether there is practical benefit |
| 3↔3 | recognition of shared pain | limited repair capacity | what actually supports repair |
| 3↔4 | different levels of attention | feeling abandoned | whether the other person understood the topic's importance |
| 4↔4 | little immediate conflict | shared neglect | which decisions are being deferred |

<!-- section:5-2-strategic-salience -->
### 5.2 Strategic Salience

Contacts involving first and third positions may be more noticeable, but this is a hypothesis rather than a fixed numerical weight.

<!-- section:5-3-tetrad-and-world-modifiers -->
### 5.3 Tetrad and “World” Modifiers

Present+Future and Past+Eternity provide different thematic languages. Similarity may mean resonance or a shared blind spot; difference may mean broader coverage or a priority conflict.

<!-- section:5-4-do-not-import-socionics-names -->
### 5.4 Do Not Import Socionics Names

Duality, Conflict, Supervision, and other names depend on Model A and do not describe a temporal-existential mechanism.

<!-- section:6-1-2-structural-description-policy -->
### 6.1.2 Structural-Description Policy

A name must be shown with its signature and mechanism. Harbor does not mean safety, Storm does not mean destruction, and Counterweights does not mean an ideal match.

<!-- section:6-2-interpretive-families -->
### 6.2 Interpretive Families

| Family | Possible resource | Possible friction |
|---|---|---|
| identity / resonance | shared temporal language | shared blind spots |
| `2↔3` support | help with a vulnerable theme | dependence or pressure |
| `1↔4` anchor | clear support | frame domination |
| `1↔3` overpressure | rapid visibility of a difference | shame and strategic rupture |
| shared 3rd | empathy | weak repair |
| shared 4th | little dispute | practical neglect |
| full inversion `4321` | broad coverage | control, dependence, misunderstanding |

Rival explanations include life stage, culture, power, attachment, time perspective, stress, shared history, and the concrete decision under discussion.

<!-- section:7-tetrad-relations -->
## 7. Tetrad Relations

<!-- section:7-1-what-a-tetrad-may-indicate -->
### 7.1 What a Tetrad May Indicate

A shared rhythm, vocabulary, and assumptions about memory, place, direction, and meaning.

<!-- section:7-2-what-a-tetrad-does-not-prove -->
### 7.2 What a Tetrad Does Not Prove

Romantic compatibility, absence of conflict, complete complementarity, or an analogue of a Socionics quadra.

<!-- section:7-3-provisional-interpretation -->
### 7.3 Provisional Interpretation

One tetrad may provide resonance and different tetrads broader coverage. Position contacts and observed behavior matter more than membership alone.

<!-- section:8-full-type-pair-matrix -->
## 8. Full Type-Pair Matrix

The 24×24 matrix contains only mechanically derived directed signatures. It is not a table of relationship quality and does not produce a verdict.

<!-- section:9-examples -->
## 9. Examples

<!-- section:9-1-epnf-fnpe -->
### 9.1 EPNF + FNPE

`4321`: reciprocal anchoring of strong and weak temporal zones is possible; check dependence, control, and repair capacity at other levels.

<!-- section:9-2-epnf-nefp -->
### 9.2 EPNF + NEFP

`3142`: pressure around Present, place, and belonging is possible; check whether defensiveness is better explained by structure, power, stress, or the concrete situation.

<!-- section:9-3-nefp-nfep -->
### 9.3 NEFP + NFEP

`1324`: shared target Present and an exchange between creative and vulnerable processing of Eternity and Future; support is possible but not guaranteed.

<!-- section:10-comparison-with-socionics-and-psychosophy -->
## 10. Comparison with Socionics and Psychosophy

Socionics models tactical information exchange, Psychosophy operational action organization, and Temporistics the strategic temporal-existential frame. They may share a position-comparison method, but not the meaning of a contact.

<!-- section:11-risks-of-importing-socionics-names -->
## 11. Risks of Importing Socionics Names

The risks are false authority, the wrong mechanism, level confusion, overprediction, and loss of Temporistics specificity.

<!-- section:12-validation-strategy -->
## 12. Validation Strategy

Test through interviews, cases, and scenarios involving relocation, career change, family tradition, crisis, loss of meaning, and plan revision. Compare a signature hypothesis with a model without Temporistics and with explanations based on Big Five, attachment, values, life stage, time perspective, conflict style, and social context. Do not introduce weights or a global score before reliable calibration.

<!-- section:13-final-position -->
## 13. Final Position

The structural layer follows exactly from the accepted type codes. The interpretive layer remains provisional and requires independent interviews, behavioral scenarios, and longitudinal testing.
