# Socionics Intertype Relations

A mechanism-first glossary of Socionics relation classes.

<!-- section:overview -->
## Overview

Socionics commonly distinguishes **14 relation classes**. Benefit and Supervision are asymmetric, so their direction must be named. In Before We Build, these relations are hypotheses about contacts between partial-model operations used in different Model A position modes. They concern only **tactical information exchange**, not forecasts of relationship, career, or marriage success. See [[socionics-reality-modeling-en]].

<!-- section:relation-types -->
## Relation Types

Each class below states a possible resource, possible friction, and what to check. A contact describes how two proposed ways of selecting and representing one situation may coordinate, translate, miss, or pressure each other; it is not a meeting of fixed personalities. Rival explanations across all classes include communication skill, shared knowledge, role, power, stress, culture, attachment, and task context.

Terms such as `strong`, `weak`, `valued`, and `vulnerable` in the mechanisms below are attributed Model A shorthand. Read them through the position-mode map, never as abilities or deficits. Any apparent relation can also change with context and the roles currently being enacted.

<!-- section:identity -->
### Identity

- **Aliases:** Identical.
- **Model A mechanism:** the same type places elements in the same function positions.
- **Possible resource:** recognizable information language and exchange of experience.
- **Possible friction:** shared blind spots or competition in the same channels.
- **What to check:** whether priorities actually match rather than merely using similar words.

<!-- section:duality -->
### Duality

- **Aliases:** Dual relation.
- **Model A mechanism:** reciprocal valued-weak complement, especially `1↔5` and `2↔6`, with relatively little direct PoLR pressure.
- **Possible resource:** each person may supply information the other values and handles less confidently.
- **Possible friction:** support may be unwanted, and closeness introduces conflicts from other levels and contexts.
- **What to check:** consent to receive support and whether the pattern persists under load.

<!-- section:activation -->
### Activation

- **Aliases:** Activity.
- **Model A mechanism:** mutual stimulation of valued super-id/mobilizing zones.
- **Possible resource:** energy and initiative.
- **Possible friction:** overstimulation or an unstable pace.
- **What to check:** what happens after the initial rise in energy.

<!-- section:mirror -->
### Mirror

- **Model A mechanism:** shared valued elements with leading/creative inversion (`1↔2`).
- **Possible resource:** complementary angles and mutual correction.
- **Possible friction:** argument about how to frame an equally important topic.
- **What to check:** whether the difference produces clarification or a struggle for the “right” frame.

<!-- section:benefit-request-social-order -->
### Benefit / Request / Social Order

- **Directional roles:** benefactor → beneficiary.
- **Model A mechanism:** asymmetric activation of a valued weak zone.
- **Possible resource:** motivation and transfer of informational initiative.
- **Possible friction:** uneven exchange and an implicit, misunderstood expectation.
- **What to check:** who initiates, who responds, and whether either person can decline without punishment.

<!-- section:supervision-revision-audit -->
### Supervision / Revision / Audit

- **Directional roles:** supervisor → supervisee.
- **Model A mechanism:** the supervisor's leading element contacts the supervisee's vulnerable/PoLR area.
- **Possible resource:** early detection of an error when feedback is invited and bounded.
- **Possible friction:** shame, defense, or a sense of constant inspection.
- **What to check:** content, tone, power, and the right to limit correction.

<!-- section:business-cooperation -->
### Business / Cooperation

- **Aliases:** Look-a-like.
- **Model A mechanism:** strong-function coordination without full relief of valued weak zones.
- **Possible resource:** task coordination and shared competence.
- **Possible friction:** missing support outside the task.
- **What to check:** whether coordination works in the actual role and after conditions change.

<!-- section:mirage-illusionary -->
### Mirage / Illusionary

- **Model A mechanism:** partial comfort with unstable valued alignment.
- **Possible resource:** ease in a bounded context.
- **Possible friction:** expectation gaps under pressure.
- **What to check:** which important themes remain unsupported.

<!-- section:quasi-identity -->
### Quasi-identity

- **Model A mechanism:** similar informational topics with different valued/unvalued arrangements.
- **Possible resource:** an alternative view of a familiar subject.
- **Possible friction:** the same words may carry different priorities.
- **What to check:** concrete examples and decisions rather than labels.

<!-- section:extinguishment-contrary -->
### Extinguishment / Contrary

- **Model A mechanism:** reciprocal damping or redirection of information initiative.
- **Possible resource:** checking an excessive impulse.
- **Possible friction:** loss of momentum or felt invalidation.
- **What to check:** whether damping is useful criticism, fatigue, or a control struggle.

<!-- section:conflict -->
### Conflict

- **Model A mechanism:** each side's confident signals contact the other's weak/unvalued zones.
- **Possible resource:** clear discovery of incompatible information requirements.
- **Possible friction:** recurrent defense at close range.
- **What to check:** distance, feedback rules, safety, and non-typological causes of conflict.

<!-- section:super-ego -->
### Super-Ego

- **Model A mechanism:** mutual contact around conscious weak/unvalued regulation zones.
- **Possible resource:** formal coordination under explicit rules.
- **Possible friction:** excessive correctness, strain, or mutual correction.
- **What to check:** whether difficulty depends on role, status, or lack of trust.

<!-- section:kindred-comparative -->
### Kindred / Comparative

- **Aliases:** Congenerity.
- **Model A mechanism:** shared leading orientation with different supporting values.
- **Possible resource:** rapid recognition of the central topic.
- **Possible friction:** disagreement about what should support it.
- **What to check:** actual solution methods rather than presumed “kinship.”

<!-- section:semi-duality -->
### Semi-duality

- **Aliases:** Partial duality.
- **Model A mechanism:** some valued weak zones are complemented but the exchange does not fully close.
- **Possible resource:** support in selected channels.
- **Possible friction:** important gaps and inflated expectations.
- **What to check:** which needs are supported and which remain unanswered.

<!-- section:canonical-label-policy -->
## Canonical Label Policy

The names in the table are the primary project labels. Request/Social Order, Revision/Audit, Cooperation/Look-a-like, Illusionary, Contrary, Comparative, and Partial duality remain searchable aliases rather than extra classes. A name is never a rating of relationship quality.
