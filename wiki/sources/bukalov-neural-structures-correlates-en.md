# Bukalov — Neural Structures as Physical Correlates of Information-Metabolism Functions

> **Provenance:** `primary-source / school publication` — a publication by an author affiliated with the International Institute of Socionics.
> **Claim status:** `speculative neuroscience hypothesis`.
> **Use policy:** it may be cited as Bukalov/IIS's position; it must not be used as independent evidence for the neuroscientific validity of Socionics or Model A.

<!-- section:what-the-source-explicitly-claims -->
## What the Source Explicitly Claims

The abstract connects neuroscientific findings about structures involved in self-awareness with a possible search for physical correlates of the Socionics information model. Bukalov presents as a research possibility the existence of special **8(16) neural structures** that might correspond to Jungian psychic functions or Aušra Augustinavičiūtė's information-metabolism functions and might eventually be detected using neurophysiology.

<!-- section:before-we-build-interpretation -->
## Before We Build Interpretation

For Before We Build this is a **neuroscience-adjacent bridge hypothesis** within one Socionics school. It documents an attempt to treat information-metabolism functions as candidates for future neurophysiological operationalization. It does not show that:

- information-metabolism functions have been localized in the brain;
- the eight information aspects have confirmed neural modules;
- Model A has independent neuroscientific validation;
- fMRI, EEG, or other physiology can determine a Socionics type.

<!-- section:neuroscience-boundary -->
## Neuroscience Boundary

Contemporary neuroscience usually treats cognition as distributed network activity rather than a simple “one typological function — one brain area” correspondence. Attention, executive control, salience, prediction, self-referential processing, and social cognition could supply comparison constructs, but each mapping requires separate operationalization.

Safe wording: Bukalov proposes possible neurocognitive or neurophysiological correlates of information-metabolism functions. These are speculative conceptual mappings, not established neural correlates.

<!-- section:what-would-be-needed-for-stronger-evidence -->
## What Would Be Needed for Stronger Evidence

1. Operationalize the functions as observable tasks or behavioral indicators.
2. Establish a reliable, independent type or functional-profile assessment.
3. Pre-register EEG/ERP, fMRI, lesion, neuropsychological, or psychophysiological hypotheses.
4. Separate effects from intelligence, working memory, stress, anxiety, fatigue, culture, and language.
5. Obtain independent replication and open data.

<!-- section:safe-claims-for-derived-pages -->
## Safe Claims for Derived Pages

Derived pages may say that Bukalov proposed a hypothesis, that the paper belongs to an IIS school research program, and that it is useful for the history of attempts to naturalize Socionics. They must not say that neuroscience has proved information-metabolism functions, found eight or sixteen Socionic structures, validated Model A biologically, or enabled brain-based typing.

## Source Assessment

<!-- section:source-claims -->
### What the Source Claims

Bukalov's work proposes correspondences between Socionics information functions, types, and neural structures.

<!-- section:source-evidence -->
### Data or Evidence Provided

The summary records the author's theoretical arguments and cited literature; it does not identify a preregistered independent replication with validated type measurement.

<!-- section:source-limitations -->
### Limitations

The evidence chain mixes model interpretation with neuroscience analogy, and source attribution does not establish a diagnostic neural marker.

<!-- section:bwb-accepts -->
### What BWB Accepts

BWB accepts the work as a traceable historical proposal and a source of narrowly testable research questions.

<!-- section:bwb-contested -->
### What Remains Contested or Open

Every specific function-to-structure correspondence and any claimed causal interpretation remain contested.

<!-- section:bwb-rejected-or-historical -->
### Rejected or Historical-Only

BWB rejects presenting these correspondences as established biomarkers, using them to type a person, or inferring pathology from a typological profile.
