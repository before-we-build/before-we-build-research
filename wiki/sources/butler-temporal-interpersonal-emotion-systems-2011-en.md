# Temporal Interpersonal Emotion Systems: The "TIES" That Form Relationships

**Author:** Emily A. Butler  
**Venue:** *Personality and Social Psychology Review*, 2011, 15 (4), pp. 367–393  
**URL:** [https://doi.org/10.1177/1088868311411164](https://doi.org/10.1177/1088868311411164)  
**Language:** English  

<!-- section:key-point-for-before-we-build -->
## Key Point for Before We Build

Emotional coordination in dyads is not a static match of traits, but a non-linear temporal dynamical system where experiential, expressive, and physiological processes become continuously coupled. Crucially, high emotional linkage or synchrony is not inherently healthy—it can reflect escalation of distress or destructive attractor dynamics, proving that compatibility requires adaptive regulation rather than simple matching.

<!-- section:main-content-of-the-article -->
## Main Content of the Article

The article integrates dynamical systems theory into relationship science through the Temporal Interpersonal Emotion Systems (TIES) framework:

1. **Dynamical Coupling across Channels:** Emotion is conceptualized as an emergent property of interdependent feedback loops operating across subjective feelings, expressive behavior, and autonomic physiology.
2. **Typology of Interpersonal Emotional Linkage:** Linkage manifests in multiple temporal patterns, including in-phase covariation, compensatory dampening, oscillatory cycles, and shared attractor states.
3. **The Non-Benevolence of Synchrony:** The author establishes that emotional linkage cannot be equated with relational well-being. Coupled emotional systems frequently trap distressed dyads in mutually reinforcing negative states.

### Research Conclusions

1. Relational outcomes must be studied as time-series interaction trajectories rather than static cross-sectional similarity.
2. Emotional co-regulation requires flexible asymmetric buffering rather than rigid synchrony.
3. The framework provides a mathematical and theoretical baseline for BWB's dynamic co-regulation model without endorsing typological determinism.

## Source Assessment

<!-- section:source-claims -->
### What the Source Claims

The author claims that interpersonal emotion is best understood as a dynamical system of temporal linkage across experience, behavior, and physiology, where stability and change are governed by systemic attractor landscapes.

<!-- section:source-evidence -->
### Data or Evidence Provided

Theoretical synthesis integrating dynamic systems mathematics, longitudinal physiological studies, and behavioral observation literature in social and clinical psychology.

<!-- section:source-limitations -->
### Limitations

The paper provides an integrative theoretical framework and mathematical taxonomy rather than a single empirical dataset; empirical verification requires complex time-series data and specialized modeling.

<!-- section:bwb-accepts -->
### What BWB Accepts

BWB accepts that dyadic compatibility is an ongoing temporal co-regulation process and that emotional linkage can be either adaptive or destructive depending on communicative repair and relational context.

<!-- section:bwb-contested -->
### What Remains Contested or Open

Whether specific typological predispositions (such as Model A aspect operations or Psychosophy positions) systematically bias dyads toward particular attractor states or dampening patterns remains an unverified empirical question.

<!-- section:bwb-rejected-or-historical -->
### Rejected or Historical-Only

BWB rejects equating emotional or physiological synchrony with relationship health, compatibility, or moral alignment.
