# The influence of psychological compatibility in professional groups on team socio-psychological climate

**Authors:** Alexander A. Volkov, Natalia N. Voloskova, Maria A. Gladkikh
**Venue:** *Humanization of Education*, 2025, No. 4, pp. 39–55
**URL:** [PDF, p. 41](https://humanization.ru/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/%D0%93%D0%9E-4-2025.pdf#page=41)
**Language:** Russian

<!-- section:key-point-for-before-we-build -->
## Key Point for Before We Build

The authors describe psychological compatibility as a multilevel phenomenon. This supplies an **external academic analogy** for BWB's resistance to a single flat score. It does not validate BWB's particular four levels or its mappings to Temporistics, Psychosophy, and Socionics.

<!-- section:main-content-of-the-article -->
## Main Content of the Article

<!-- section:1-traditional-levels-in-academic-psychology -->
### 1. Traditional levels in academic psychology

The article organizes prior literature into psychophysiological factors (tempo, nervous-system and sensorimotor differences), psychological/personality factors (motives, behavior, cognitive styles), and socio-psychological factors (values, meanings, attitudes, and role expectations).

<!-- section:2-similarity-versus-complementarity -->
### 2. Similarity versus complementarity

The authors associate similarity more strongly with goals, values, and norms, and complementarity with the distribution of roles, pace, and psychological resources. These are source claims about professional groups, not universal relationship laws.

<!-- section:3-team-climate -->
### 3. Team climate

The article links compatibility with trust, perceived safety, support, satisfaction, conflict, and productivity. Its scope is professional-group climate; causal direction, measurement quality, and transfer to couples require separate evaluation.

### Research Conclusions

3. It does not establish BWB's ontology, type systems, or predictive validity.
