# Acute Stress Impacts Executive-Social Function: Evidence From Prefrontal Activation and fNIRS-Based Hyperscanning

**Authors:** Zhihua Guo, Yue Gong, Liu Yang, Y. Li, R. Qiu, Xia Zhu  
**Venue:** *Brain and Behavior*, 2026, e70231  
**URL:** [https://doi.org/10.1002/brb3.70231](https://doi.org/10.1002/brb3.70231)  
**Language:** English  

<!-- section:key-point-for-before-we-build -->
## Key Point for Before We Build

Under acute stress, executive control and social interaction mechanisms couple into a single functional system in dyads. This provides an external neuroimaging foundation for BWB's thesis that compatibility is not an invariant static trait match, but a dynamic capacity for mutual co-regulation, conversational repair, and role flexibility under pressure.

<!-- section:main-content-of-the-article -->
## Main Content of the Article

<!-- section:1-executive-social-coupling-under-stress -->
### 1. Executive–Social Function Coupling Under Stress

The authors propose the *Executive–Social Function Coupling Hypothesis*: acute stress bridges cognitive control systems (dlPFC) and social-communicative mechanisms (IFG, perspective-taking). Rather than operating as isolated cognitive modules, cognitive control and interpersonal coordination become interdependent when couples or dyads face external threat or acute task load.

<!-- section:2-fnirs-hyperscanning-and-prefrontal-activation -->
### 2. fNIRS Hyperscanning and Prefrontal Activation

Using dual-head functional near-infrared spectroscopy (fNIRS), the study simultaneously measured oxygenated hemoglobin (HbO) across prefrontal cortical regions in dyads performing cooperative problem-solving. Acute stress significantly increased metabolic demands in both the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (dlPFC) and inferior frontal gyrus (IFG).

<!-- section:3-inter-brain-synchrony-and-coordination-breakdown -->
### 3. Inter-Brain Synchrony and Coordination Breakdown

Cooperative dyads exhibited heightened inter-brain synchrony (IBS) in prefrontal regions when successfully maintaining communicative pacing and mutual adjustment. When acute stress overwhelmed cognitive capacity or led to rigid, unilateral decision-making, neural synchrony deteriorated, leading to communicative and behavioral breakdown.

<!-- section:research-conclusions -->
### Research Conclusions

1. Dyadic compatibility must be investigated under cognitive load and acute stress, not merely in resting self-report states.
2. Inter-brain synchrony reflects dynamic communicative co-regulation, supporting BWB's focus on turn-by-turn repair over static compatibility scores.
3. The findings do not validate Socionics Model A, Psychosophy orders, or any discrete typological structure.

<!-- section:source-assessment -->
## Source Assessment

<!-- section:source-claims -->
### What the Source Claims

The authors claim that acute stress couples executive control and social cognition in dyads, and that prefrontal activation and inter-brain synchrony reflect dyadic adaptation to acute challenge.

<!-- section:source-evidence -->
### Data or Evidence Provided

Dual-brain fNIRS recordings (HbO concentrations) during cooperative behavioral tasks under controlled acute stress and non-stress conditions in adult dyads.

<!-- section:source-limitations -->
### Limitations

Laboratory-induced acute stress may differ from chronic relational, economic, or existential stress; fNIRS measures hemodynamic response rather than direct neuronal firing; dyadic tasks do not equate to long-term intimate relationships.

<!-- section:bwb-accepts -->
### What BWB Accepts

BWB accepts that executive control and social communication are inextricably linked under pressure, and that dyadic coordination under stress is an emergent, real-time process.

<!-- section:bwb-contested -->
### What Remains Contested or Open

Whether specific typological dispositions (e.g. information aspects or action preferences) reliably predict distinct neural synchrony patterns under stress remains completely unverified.

<!-- section:bwb-rejected-or-historical -->
### Rejected or Historical-Only

BWB rejects treating inter-brain synchrony as a biological matchmaking score or using this paper to claim neurobiological proof for Socionics, Psychosophy, or Temporistics.
