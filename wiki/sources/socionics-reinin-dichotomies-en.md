# Socionics Reinin Dichotomies

<!-- section:overview -->
## Overview

Reinin dichotomies are an additional layer of type splits beyond the four Jungian dichotomies. Their usefulness and even validity are disputed across Socionics schools.

<!-- section:classical-wikisocion-position -->
## Classical / Wikisocion Position

Wikisocion treats Reinin dichotomies as a recognized classical Socionics layer:

- 15 dichotomies total
- 4 Jungian foundation pairs
- 11 derived non-Jungian pairs

Wikisocion also explicitly notes that the usefulness of many or most Reinin dichotomies is questioned by many socionists.

For `SLI`, Wikisocion lists:

- judicious
- objectivist
- aristocratic
- result
- carefree
- obstinate
- dynamic
- strategic
- constructivist
- positivist
- asking

<!-- section:imperative-socionics-position -->
## Imperative Socionics Position

Imperative Socionics challenges part of the classical Reinin set.

In the source snippets currently available to the project, it treats at least the following as **false traits**:

- aristocracy / democracy
- process / result
- asking / declaring
- positivism / negativism

This means these traits should not be treated as universally valid across schools.

<!-- section:practical-project-rule -->
## Practical Project Rule

- Use the Jungian foundation with the highest confidence.
- Use classical Reinin traits only with explicit confidence labels.
- Mark school-disputed traits as `school-dependent` or `contested`.
- Do not let Reinin traits override clearer Model A evidence.

<!-- section:recommended-confidence-split -->
## Recommended Confidence Split

<!-- section:high-confidence -->
### High confidence

- introvert / extravert
- irrational / rational
- logic / ethics
- sensing / intuition

<!-- section:medium-confidence -->
### Medium confidence

- traits directly listed in a classical table for a type and not strongly contradicted in current use

<!-- section:contested -->
### Contested

- traits explicitly challenged by another school in the current source set

<!-- section:source-assessment -->
## Source Assessment

<!-- section:source-claims -->
### What the Source Claims

Socionics sources derive Reinin dichotomies from combinations of Jungian dichotomies and propose group-level behavioral meanings for them.

<!-- section:source-evidence -->
### Data or Evidence Provided

The page records formal derivations, names, and attributed descriptions; it does not provide a sufficiently replicated validation dataset.

<!-- section:source-limitations -->
### Limitations

Multiple comparisons, unstable operational definitions, school differences, typing error, and small or selected samples can create apparent patterns.

<!-- section:bwb-accepts -->
### What BWB Accepts

BWB accepts the formal definitions as historical model content and as candidates for carefully preregistered tests.

<!-- section:bwb-contested -->
### What Remains Contested or Open

Reliability, factorial structure, discriminant validity, and reproducibility of individual dichotomies remain contested.

<!-- section:bwb-rejected-or-historical -->
### Rejected or Historical-Only

BWB rejects assigning character, competence, morality, or relationship outcome from one Reinin label or treating formal derivation as empirical proof.
