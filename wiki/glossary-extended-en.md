# Extended Research Glossary

<!-- section:in-90-seconds -->
## In 90 seconds

These terms are easy to read in more than one way. Active pages should qualify the intended meaning at first use.

<!-- section:definition-and-scope -->
## Model, function, aspect, and position

**Model** may mean a formal representation, an internal information model, Socionics Model A, or a statistical model. Name which one.

**Function** may mean a proposed psychic function, mathematical mapping, or software operation. Name the discipline.

**Aspect** is a system-specific dimension in a source typology. In the BWB Socionics reconstruction it is a proposed content-operation pair; aspects with similar names across systems are not automatically equivalent.

**Position** is a location in a type structure. In Model A it is kept distinct from the aspect and interpreted as a proposed use mode. Any cognitive interpretation assigned by BWB must be labelled a project hypothesis rather than a canonical alias.

<!-- section:shared-example -->
## Example of disambiguation

“Future predicts action” is ambiguous. It might refer to a Temporistics aspect, a future-time score, a scenario variable, or ordinary expectations. A research sentence must name the construct, instrument, population, and outcome.

<!-- section:observations -->
## Construct and measurement

**Construct** — a deliberately defined concept intended for description or measurement.

**Operationalization** — rules connecting a construct to tasks, items, indicators, coding, or outcomes.

**Scale score** — an instrument output calculated according to a declared scoring key; it is not automatically the construct itself.

**Measurement error** — variation due to the instrument or procedure rather than the intended construct.

<!-- section:hypotheses -->
## Reliability and validity

**Reliability** — consistency under specified conditions; it does not establish that the intended construct is measured.

**Content validity** — whether items adequately represent the construct domain.

**Convergent validity** — expected association with relevant measures.

**Discriminant validity** — empirical distinction from rival constructs.

**Predictive validity** — performance on a prespecified future outcome.

<!-- section:alternatives -->
## Model alternatives and how to compare them

Before preferring a typological explanation, compare it with role, learned skill, language, culture, health, stress, response style, and a simpler established construct. The following terms describe tests that can distinguish competing models:

**Measurement invariance** — evidence that measurement behaves comparably across groups or occasions.

**Calibration** — agreement between predicted uncertainty or probability and observed frequency; discrimination alone is insufficient.

**Incremental validity** — improvement beyond a simpler or already established model.

**Replication** — a new test of a claim under declared conditions, not reuse of the development data.

<!-- section:non-inferences -->
## Inference and uncertainty

**Correlation** — association; not by itself causation.

**Causal mechanism** — a claim about how change is produced; stronger than a latent-process story.

**Uncertainty** — limits arising from sampling, measurement, missingness, model choice, and unknown conditions.

**Falsification condition** — an observation or pattern declared in advance as challenging a hypothesis.

<!-- section:conversation-questions -->
## Source-and-norm vocabulary and practical questions

**Source tradition** — the original and later literature of a typological system; it may contain disagreement.

**Raw source** — preserved input material, not an endorsed BWB conclusion.

**Source attribution** — a claim about what a source says, distinct from project acceptance.

**Normative source** — an authority or framework used to determine what ought to govern an application.

- What exactly does the source claim, and what evidence does it report?
- Is the current sentence source attribution or BWB acceptance?
- Which rival construct or contextual explanation has been tested?
- Which explicit normative source governs what ought to be done in this application?

<!-- section:researcher-route -->
## Research route for pair-level claims

**Individual-level variable** describes one person. **Pair-level variable** describes a relation, interaction, agreement, complementarity, or joint outcome. The former cannot be relabelled as the latter without a defined composition model.

**Resource/friction profile** is a qualitative map of possibilities to investigate. **Decision support** organizes evidence for human judgement; it is not automated decision replacement.
