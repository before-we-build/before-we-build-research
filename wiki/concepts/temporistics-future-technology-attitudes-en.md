# Attitudes Toward Technology and Future Positions in Temporistics

English · [[temporistics-future-technology-attitudes-ru|Русский]] · [[temporistics-future-technology-attitudes-uk|Українська]]

<!-- section:in-90-seconds -->
## Summary in 90 Seconds

This page articulates a Before We Build research hypothesis linking the positional modes of the "Future" aspect in Temporistics to baseline attitudes toward technological progress:

- **1st Future (1F / Captain): Projective techno-enthusiasm.** Technology is perceived as an active instrument for constructing a desired reality, scaling intention, and overcoming environmental constraints.
- **4th Future (4F / Passenger): Delegating techno-optimism.** Technology is viewed as the objective march of progress; trusting automation and algorithms removes the burden of personal scenario planning.
- **2nd Future (2F / Steersman): Instrumental techno-skepticism.** Technology is treated as a flexible yet limited tool for step-by-step navigation; skepticism is directed against dogmatic hype and teleological inevitability.
- **3rd Future (3F / Stowaway): Existential techno-skepticism and risk auditing.** Heightened sensitivity to vulnerabilities, systemic hazards, loss of agency, dehumanization, and technological catastrophes; acute vulnerability regarding humanity's place in the arriving future.

This is an exploratory hypothesis about strategic temporal framing, not a diagnostic classification, moral verdict, or deterministic prediction.

<!-- section:definition-and-scope -->
## Definition and Scope

Within Before We Build, Temporistics operates at the **strategic level of compatibility**, modeling latent processes of scenario-based, prognostic temporal structuring.

The **Future (F)** aspect addresses trajectory, openness of options, probabilistic forecasting, and directional transformation ("Where are we heading?"). Technological innovations (automation, artificial intelligence, biotechnology) act as prominent external catalysts demanding an interpretive stance toward the temporal horizon.

**Scope boundaries:**
1. **Separation from the value-moral foundation:** The ethical legitimacy of technologies, human dignity, and non-negotiable safety boundaries are normative questions belonging to the foundational level, not temporistics.
2. **Separation from the tactical level (Socionics):** Technical execution, computational efficiency, and practical workflows ($Te$ / business logic) are tactical information operations, distinct from strategic temporal horizons.
3. **Separation from the operational level (Psychosophy):** Epistemic doubt in 3rd Logic or vigilance in 3rd Will concern judgment and agency, whereas 3rd Future specifically monitors existential vulnerability along the temporal trajectory.

<!-- section:shared-example -->
## Shared Example

A collaborative team or couple considers integrating an autonomous AI workflow into their shared long-term endeavors.

- **A partner with 1F** becomes energized: they immediately envision leaping past current resource bottlenecks, reshaping operations, and expanding the project's ambition ("this propels us ten years ahead").
- **A partner with 4F** readily welcomes it: delegating cognitive forecasting to an external reliable system feels natural and frictionless ("progress is unstoppable; the algorithm will optimize it without our constant intervention").
- **A partner with 2F** engages in pragmatic calibration: suggesting a localized pilot test, assessing side effects, and maintaining manual fallback routes ("it is a helpful tool for discrete steps, but let's avoid anchoring the entire venture to it").
- **A partner with 3F** experiences acute apprehension: what happens if the system fails or hallucinates? Will core competencies atrophy? Who remains accountable? Deep anxiety surfaces over forfeiting control over the collective trajectory.

<!-- section:observations -->
## Direct Observations

In real-world interaction, observable divergence centers on emotional and regulatory responses rather than raw technical expertise:

1. **Reaction to technological hype:** 1F assimilates the vision into self-directed goals; 4F absorbs it as an ambient baseline; 2F disaggregates functional utility from marketing claims; 3F actively interrogates failure modes and systemic liabilities.
2. **Reaction to technological breakdowns:** 1F experiences irritation at momentum interruption; 4F feels disoriented when external infrastructure stumbles; 2F calmly pivots to alternative tools; 3F feels their core misgivings validated ("we should never have depended on this").
3. **Focal points in foresight discourse:** 1F emphasizes transformative agency; 4F highlights convenience and systemic continuity; 2F emphasizes social adaptiveness and incremental course correction; 3F fixates on existential risks (AI alignment, alienation, civilizational fragility).

## Positional Modes and Cognitive Analogies

In the BWB cognitive resource routing framework, four-position structures align with baseline behavioral regulation systems:

| Position | Aspect Mode | Cognitive-Motivational Analogy | Attitude Toward Technology |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1F (Captain)** | Autonomous goal-setting | **Novelty Seeking / BAS (Approach)** | **Projective techno-enthusiasm:** technology as a lever of will and catalyst of transformative future-building. |
| **2F (Steersman)** | Dialogic and procedural | **Reward Dependence / Plasticity** | **Instrumental techno-skepticism:** technology as a practical tool requiring perpetual human calibration and bounded reliance. |
| **3F (Stowaway)** | Risk and error monitoring | **Harm Avoidance / BIS (Inhibition)** | **Existential techno-skepticism:** technology as a proliferation of uncontained hazards, displacement, and systemic vulnerability. |
| **4F (Passenger)** | External frame delegation | **Low Reactivity / Resource Conservation** | **Delegating techno-optimism:** technology as ambient progress, relieving the cognitive burden of forward planning. |

<!-- section:alternatives -->
## Alternative Explanations

Observed attitudes toward technology may stem from non-typological determinants:

- **Professional background:** A software architect recognizes model limitations through domain mastery rather than typological disposition.
- **Generational cohort:** Digital natives naturally inhabit technological environments differently than individuals socialized in analog settings.
- **Trait neuroticism (Big Five):** Generalized emotional reactivity projects onto technological uncertainty.
- **Normative and worldview commitments:** Religious, philosophical, or ecological convictions regarding human stewardship directly establish technological boundaries.

<!-- section:non-inferences -->
## Non-Inferences

- A person's temporistic type cannot be deduced solely from their enthusiasm or skepticism regarding a specific technology.
- 3rd Future skepticism must not be conflated with Luddism, backwardness, or lack of sophistication; in collective decision-making, it provides indispensable pre-emptive risk auditing.
- 1st and 4th Future enthusiasm cannot be assumed to denote recklessness, naivety, or irresponsibility.
- Stances toward technology do not establish moral worth, character, vocational capability, or relational compatibility scores.

<!-- section:conversation-questions -->
## Practical Conversation Questions

1. Which technological advancements evoke hope versus anxiety, and what underlying assumptions about the future drive those reactions?
2. When we debate automating a workflow, are we disagreeing on operational utility or on our tolerance for uncertainty and external dependence?
3. Are we inadvertently abdicating strategic responsibility by over-relying on algorithmic inertia?
4. How can we integrate bold projective vision (1F/4F resources) with rigorous vulnerability auditing and procedural prudence (2F/3F resources)?

<!-- section:researcher-route -->
## Researcher Route and Falsification

Empirical verification requires disciplined operationalization:
1. **Experimental design:** Presenting standardized technological adoption scenarios with structured payoff/risk matrices to cohorts with independently verified Temporistics profiles.
2. **Covariate control:** Rigorously controlling for domain expertise, age, and Big Five personality traits (specifically Openness and Neuroticism).
3. **Falsification condition:** The hypothesis is falsified if, under covariate controls, variance in techno-enthusiasm and techno-skepticism displays no statistically significant association with Future aspect positioning.
