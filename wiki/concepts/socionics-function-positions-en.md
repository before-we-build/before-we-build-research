# Socionics Function Positions

English · [[socionics-function-positions-ru|Русский]] · [[socionics-function-positions-uk|Українська]]

<!-- section:overview -->
## Overview

An aspect proposes which distinctions a partial representation preserves and which modeling operation it performs. A Model A position proposes how that operation may be used. A position is therefore not an aspect, topic, skill, or amount of ability. See [[socionics-reality-modeling-en]].

<!-- section:position-by-position-summary -->
## Eight proposed position modes

| Position | Traditional name | Revisable BWB mode | Verification focus |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Leading / Base | stable foreground framing | Does the operation recurrently organize the initial representation? |
| 2 | Creative | flexible situational production | Does the operation adapt output to changing task demands? |
| 3 | Role | norm-guided, effortful application | Does use follow learned expectations, and when does that explanation fail? |
| 4 | Vulnerable / PoLR | least-stable modeling under pressure | Which errors recur under comparable pressure, and do they disappear with training or support? |
| 5 | Suggestive | valued, externally scaffolded intake | Is external framing repeatedly sought or merely convenient in this context? |
| 6 | Mobilizing / Activation | supported or activated development | Does supported practice change use across time and tasks? |
| 7 | Ignoring | background monitoring | Is the operation detected without becoming the foreground frame? |
| 8 | Demonstrative | automatic background production | Does the operation produce unprompted output outside the declared focus? |

<!-- section:source-properties -->
## Attributed source properties

Socionics schools also classify positions with `mental/vital`, `accepting/producing`, `strong/weak`, `valued/unvalued`, `inert/contact`, and dimensionality labels. Those are Model A claims to operationalize and test. They do not become measurements merely because the table is internally regular.

| Source property | Positions in the common school layout |
|---|---|
| Mental / Vital | `1–4` / `5–8` |
| Accepting / Producing | `1,3,5,7` / `2,4,6,8` |
| “Strong” / “Weak” | `1,2,7,8` / `3,4,5,6` |
| Valued / Unvalued | `1,2,5,6` / `3,4,7,8` |

<!-- section:dimensionality -->
## Dimensionality boundary

The common `4D: 1,8; 3D: 2,7; 2D: 3,6; 1D: 4,5` layout is retained as attributed school terminology. BWB does not translate it into intelligence, competence, task performance, or a general capacity score. Validation would require independent task measures, model comparison, reliability, invariance, and held-out prediction.

<!-- section:dimensionality-in-plain-language -->
## Dimensionality in plain language

Imagine an aspect operation as an internal navigator: it selects relevant features, compresses them into a working map, proposes a next step, and updates the map after feedback. In BWB's cautious research translation, dimensionality is a hypothesis about **how broadly and reliably that map transfers across conditions**:

- `1D` — the map is narrowest and more often needs a familiar example or external scaffold;
- `2D` — the map can use learned rules and supported practice;
- `3D` — the map is proposed to adapt more flexibly to the current situation;
- `4D` — the map is proposed to preserve the broadest and most stable contour as situation, time, and feedback change.

This is not an official decoding into four measured quantities or a ladder of human worth. A familiar route can be navigated better with `1D` than an unfamiliar route with `4D`: learning, experience, state, and task conditions may outweigh the proposed position pattern.

<!-- section:same-life-example -->
## One life example: a shared trip

Two people are planning a weekend trip. For illustration, take the proposed `Te` operation: linking actions and methods to observable results and revising procedure through feedback. The table does not describe eight kinds of traveler. It gives eight **discriminating hypotheses** that would need testing across several comparable trips.

| Position | What the operation might look like | What to test instead of concluding from one episode |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 (`4D`) | The person builds the plan around testability: travel time, cost, and reliability become a stable foreground frame. | Does this organizing pattern persist with another country, budget, and group? |
| 8 (`4D`) | Logistics quietly become a workable plan even though the person does not make efficiency the main topic. | Does this background output arise without prompting, and is professional experience a better explanation? |
| 2 (`3D`) | When a train is cancelled, the person rapidly compares options and rebuilds the route around new constraints. | Does flexible adaptation recur in new tasks rather than only on a familiar route? |
| 7 (`3D`) | The person notices that the route has become inefficient but may not intervene until it matters. | Is there stable background monitoring without making it the main criterion? |
| 3 (`2D`) | The person carefully follows a checklist or accepted booking method and becomes noticeably strained when the rule does not fit. | Does effort disappear after training, and how well does the rule transfer to a new setting? |
| 6 (`2D`) | With a shared sheet, feedback, and support, the person becomes more confident comparing options and learns to manage part of the plan. | Does development persist without support and transfer between trips? |
| 4 (`1D`) | Under pressure, conflicting prices, connections, and refund terms may break down the working picture of which option is actually reliable. | Does the pattern recur under comparable pressure and remain after a clear instruction? |
| 5 (`1D`) | An external frame is especially useful: advice from a knowledgeable partner, a prepared route, or a clear account of what will work. | Is this a recurrent search for scaffolding or simply sensible delegation in one unfamiliar task? |

Equal dimensionality does not make positions equal: `1` and `8` are both labeled `4D`, but the first proposes foreground organization while the second proposes background production. Likewise, `4` and `5` are both labeled `1D`, but the first is tested through modeling stability under pressure and the second through the pattern of taking in external scaffolding.

<!-- section:observations-and-alternatives -->
## Observations and alternatives

Record what is selected, preserved after compression, inferred, produced, and updated after feedback. Position evidence requires repeated comparable contexts. Expertise, instruction, language, culture, role, health, sensory access, motivation, stress, and familiarity can imitate any proposed mode.

<!-- section:non-inferences -->
## What positions do not establish

- Positions 1/2/7/8 are not ready-made abilities or virtues.
- Positions 3/4/5/6 are not deficiencies, diagnoses, or dependence scores.
- A “vulnerable” position does not prove humiliation, dislike, or inability.
- A “background” position does not prove hidden expertise.
- A position pattern does not establish innateness, neural localization, personality, vocation, morality, or relationship fate.

<!-- section:researcher-route -->
## Researcher route

First gather aspect-operation evidence through selection, compression, inference, and updating tasks. Then test position-mode predictions independently across repeated settings. Rank a Model A stack only when both layers have sufficient, discriminating evidence and rival explanations have been checked.
