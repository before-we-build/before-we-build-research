# Interaction Formats for Proposed Socionics Relations

This page neither ranks pairs nor assigns distance by type. It translates traditional relation names into hypotheses about communication formats worth testing. Family, friendship, work, mentoring, and limited contact are evaluated through actual duties, consent, behaviour, and outcomes.

<!-- section:1-choosing-a-format -->
## 1. Choosing a format

First describe the task: close daily life, a joint decision, project, learning, crisis, or occasional contact. Then record:

- what information each person must receive and transmit;
- which roles and authority are set by context;
- where errors recur;
- whether format translation, training, or distance helps;
- whether each person may refuse and revise the contact;
- whether dignity, safety, and reciprocity remain intact.

The typological name is only one rival hypothesis.

<!-- section:2-qualitative-relation-map -->
## 2. Qualitative relation map

| Traditional name | Possible format to test | Possible resource | Possible friction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Duality | joint task with explicit role rotation | different information contributions | dependency and idealization of complementarity |
| Activation | short intensive cycles with rest | energy and quick engagement | overload and haste |
| Identity | mutual learning with external checking | shared language | shared blind spots |
| Mirror | joint analysis with divided domains | different emphases in a related model | competition over final interpretation |
| Semi-duality | collaboration with an explicit unsupported-needs list | partial complementarity | expectation of complete understanding |
| Business | project with clear outputs and owners | practical division | overly functional contact |
| Kindred | parallel tasks and outside resources | similar leading focus | duplication and shared omissions |
| Mirage | informal contact plus a separate protocol for difficult decisions | ease in part of communication | errors under load |
| Quasi-identity | written definitions and criteria | familiar topic | different meanings of similar words |
| Extinguishment | separate work with integration points | correction of momentum | loss of pace and initiative |
| Benefit/request | bounded task with an explicit right to refuse | directional request | hidden hierarchy and one-sided burden |
| Supervision/revision | checking against an agreed checklist | detection of errors | chronic correction of the person rather than the task |
| Superego | formal protocol and short feedback cycles | contrasting representations | fatigue and mutual criticism |
| Conflict | translator, distance, narrow task, and stop rules | exposure of nonmatching models | repeated pressure and defensive response |

The table does not predict outcomes or claim that a relation is experienced identically in both directions.

<!-- section:3-what-to-test-in-reality -->
## 3. What to test in reality

| Question | Observable data |
|---|---|
| Did understanding become more accurate? | restatement of the other's view, corrected errors, alignment of the final decision |
| Did pressure decrease? | ability to pause and refuse, absence of punishment, both people's reports |
| Do roles work? | task completion, workload balance, hand-off quality |
| Does the effect persist? | repeated episodes and different contexts, not one successful conversation |
| Is there a rival explanation? | experience, skill, language, hierarchy, resources, health, conflict history |

<!-- section:4-family-and-closeness -->
## 4. Family and closeness

No relation name is an indication or contraindication for family. Close relationships especially require free consent, honesty, ability to repair harm, accountable power, compatible essential commitments, and demonstrated stability of agreements. Pleasant communication does not compensate for danger; translation difficulty does not prove moral incompatibility.

<!-- section:5-limits-and-revision -->
## 5. Limits and revision

If a proposed format does not improve accuracy, reciprocity, and repair, change it regardless of the typological story. If coercion, violence, exploitation, or fear of refusal is present, ordinary format experiments stop and safety takes priority.
