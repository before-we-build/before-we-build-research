# Guide to Distinguishing Typologies

A type in BWB is a model hypothesis about a pattern of perception and experience organization, not a personality type. Perception here includes selection, ordering, interpretation, and process organization rather than sensory perception alone. See [[typology-reconceptualization-en]].

The same word in Socionics, Psychosophy, and Temporistics does not automatically denote the same construct. Before We Build models three proposed process domains separately:

- **Socionics / tactical level:** hypotheses about information modelling.
- **Psychosophy / operational level:** hypotheses about analysis and synthesis in action.
- **Temporistics / strategic level:** hypotheses about abductive, inductive, and deductive organization of temporal and existential experience.

These mappings are the project's working architecture, not proof that the three systems are empirically independent.

<!-- section:1-false-correspondences -->
## 1. False correspondences

| Source term | Similar term | Why they must not be merged |
|---|---|---|
| **Will** in Psychosophy | **Volitional sensing / Se** in Socionics | The former concerns a proposed way of organizing decision ownership and action; the latter is an information aspect of force, boundaries, and mobilization in the Socionics tradition. |
| **Emotion** in Psychosophy | **Fe / Fi** in Socionics | The former concerns the place of the emotional domain in an action frame; Fe and Fi are different information aspects in the Socionics model. |
| **Logic** in Psychosophy | **Te / Ti** in Socionics | Sharing an everyday word does not establish identity between an operational construct and information aspects. |
| **Physics** in Psychosophy | **Si / Se** in Socionics | Material embodiment of action and modelling sensory information require different indicators. |
| **Future** in Temporistics | **Ni** in Socionics | A temporal aspect of life direction and a Socionics information aspect of time belong to different models. |

<!-- section:2-illustrative-combinations -->
## 2. Illustrative combinations

The following examples show logical possibility, not verified descriptions of people:

- an LII hypothesis in Socionics is compatible with any Emotion position in Psychosophy: a rigorous analytic style alone establishes nothing about organization of the emotional domain in action;
- an SLE hypothesis is compatible with any Will position: attention to force and boundaries in a situation does not establish stable ownership of life decisions;
- an ILI hypothesis is compatible with any Future position: modelling event dynamics does not establish an existential priority of the future.

Empirical use would require independently obtained results, observable indicators, rival explanations, and an uncertainty assessment.

<!-- section:3-practical-check -->
## 3. Practical check

First ask what was observed:

1. Processing and transmitting concrete information — a possible tactical question.
2. Ownership of a decision, resource, or execution — a possible operational question.
3. Connections among past, present, future, and enduring meaning — a possible strategic question.
4. Duty, dignity, consent, or safety — the value-moral foundation, not a typology.

Then name at least one contextual explanation: role, skill, culture, language, stress, health, power, or available resources. If the question still fits more than one level, preserve the ambiguity rather than forcing a classification.

<!-- section:4-inference-limits -->
## 4. Inference limits

A type pattern is not an observed fact or a kind of person. It does not determine character, morality, competence, diagnosis, values, or relationship outcome. Similar words and correlations between results do not prove identity or causation.
