# Methodology Audit for Public-Figure Research

<!-- section:conclusion -->
## Conclusion

No reviewed online test, community instrument, or text-analysis method establishes a Socionics, Psychosophy, or Temporistics code for a public figure from an open biography. The most cautious route is a multi-source corpus, exact observations, independent blind coding, alternative checks, and human arbitration.

BWB's value-moral foundation is not typed at all. Faith, conduct, consent, safety, and responsibility are considered directly rather than inferred from the three typological systems.

<!-- section:what-the-reviewed-approaches-show -->
## What the reviewed approaches show

| Approach or source | Useful contribution | What cannot be transferred |
|---|---|---|
| Wikisocion: diagnosis | warns about recognition from descriptions and ambiguity in written tests | a type description is not a diagnostic criterion |
| Sociotype.com | shows common online questions and profiles | a test result neither validates a system nor applies to a third party |
| AIM / shortened Talanov inventory | offers scale language instead of one guessed label | scales cannot be completed for a public figure from biography |
| Attitudinal Psyche | provides modern adjacent terminology for positions and axes | AP and Afanasyev's model should not be treated as one source |
| repository Psychosophy materials | preserve process/result, confidence/vulnerability, and autonomy/adaptation as questions | community questionnaires lack sufficient independent validation here |
| Temporistics specification | proposes distinguishing aspects and positional modes in scenarios | mentioning future or eternity does not establish a position |
| NLP/LLM papers 2110.01186, 2310.10679, 2403.07581, 2406.17287, 2605.17355 | show that text features and assessment of other constructs depend on corpus, prompt, genre, and model | Big Five or automatic-personality-prediction results do not validate BWB typologies or authorize predicting a person |

<!-- section:permissible-use-matrix -->
## Permissible-use matrix

| Material or method | Permissible use | Required caveat |
|---|---|---|
| official biography | roles, dates, trajectory | high role-overfit risk |
| full interview or Q&A | episodes of choice, disagreement, and correction | self-presentation and interviewer influence |
| sermon or lecture series | compare explanatory structure across genres and years | topic and church form may imitate an aspect |
| books and articles | analyze conceptual organization | editing and professional specialization |
| voluntary test or self-report | participant's own words as one source | wording effects; independent review needed |
| typological axes | construct differentiating questions before discussing a code | project hypotheses, not established latent processes |
| independent coders | test reproducibility of observations | shared training may create shared bias |
| role and genre critic | seek a simpler explanation | sceptical rhetoric cannot replace evidence |

<!-- section:role-of-ai -->
## Role of AI

AI may:

- summarize material already selected by a person, with links;
- organize verified excerpts into table fields;
- suggest alternative explanations and questions for manual checking.

AI is not a typist, confidence arbiter, behavioural predictor, or source of quotations. A person checks every excerpt against the primary source. Genre, translation, authorship, editing, and montage are recorded explicitly.

<!-- section:system-specific-warnings -->
## System-specific warnings

- Socionics: do not infer a code from an MBTI-like label, occupation, or recognition in a description.
- Psychosophy: do not infer First Volition from leadership, First Logic from education, Emotion from eloquence, or Physics from a craft.
- Temporistics: do not infer Eternity from Christian vocabulary, Future from mission, or Past from a historical topic.
- Across systems: code observations before type and retain non-typological alternatives.

<!-- section:application-to-the-current-corpus -->
## Application to the current corpus

Office remains a strong alternative explanation for union leaders. Serhii Sannikov's written corpus is useful for studying argument, but not live disagreement. Mykola Romaniuk requires multiple preaching and conversational genres. Ihor Bandura requires materials outside spiritual fatherhood. Temporistics candidates for all figures are especially tentative because the external methodology base is thin.
