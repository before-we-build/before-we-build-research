# Comparison of Christian Spiritual-Maturity Models

This page compares biblical images, discipleship models, spiritual-formation traditions, and developmental psychology of faith. It does not combine them into one scale or turn any external model into a marriage-readiness test.

<!-- section:1-biblical-reference-points -->
## 1. Biblical reference points

| Texts | Central image | Possible BWB question | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hebrews 5:11–14; 1 Corinthians 3:1–3 | infancy, mature discernment, struggle with fleshly conduct | is the ability to discern and apply teaching growing? | the metaphor does not define a complete rank of persons |
| 1 John 2:12–14 | children, young people, fathers | how are knowledge of the Father, abiding Word, and endurance expressed? | groups must not become a mechanical marriage ranking |
| Ephesians 4:11–16 | maturity of the body, truth in love | does the person build community and resist false teaching? | maturity is not individualistic and requires church context |
| Galatians 5:22–23; 2 Peter 1:5–8 | fruit of the Spirit and growing virtues | what fruit is visible over time and after conflict? | one episode or self-report cannot establish fruit |

Biblical texts are normative sources for the Christian application, not psychometric validation of an instrument.

<!-- section:2-external-models -->
## 2. External models

| Source | Main idea | Possible contribution | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jim Putman / Discipleship.org | dead, infant, child, young adult, parent | language for differing discipleship needs | popular ministry model; must not judge salvation or worth |
| Dallas Willard | transformation of heart, will, and habitual action into Christlikeness | attention to centre, practice, and love rather than knowledge alone | not a staged measurement scheme |
| GotQuestions | Christlikeness, obedience, disciplines, action of the Spirit | process and incompleteness of growth | popular secondary source, not a research method |
| Purgative / illuminative / unitive way | cleansing, illumination, union | historical language of spiritual movement | confession-specific; not a Sola Scriptura-first public basis |
| James Fowler | development of ways of construing faith | distinction between conventional and personally appropriated religiosity | developmental faith psychology is not Christian maturity |

Similar words across models do not prove identical stages or causal mechanisms.

<!-- section:3-what-may-be-observed -->
## 3. What may be observed

For Christian discernment BWB uses several evidence classes rather than a rank:

- professed foundation and choices when it becomes costly;
- openness to Scripture, correction, repentance, and counsel;
- recurring fruit, self-control, truthfulness, and love;
- responsibility and repair after harm;
- sustained church relationships and a checkable reputation;
- absence of spiritual authority used for coercion.

No class gives complete access to a person's heart. Time, context, several witnesses, and visible uncertainty are required.

<!-- section:4-rival-explanations -->
## 4. Rival explanations

Observed immaturity may reflect new faith, missing instruction, illness, trauma, cultural distance, or reaction to spiritual abuse. Religious vocabulary and activity may conversely conceal control or impression management. These possibilities must be investigated rather than closed with a stage label.

<!-- section:5-bwb-recommendation -->
## 5. BWB recommendation

In user-facing language, use four areas of inquiry—professed foundation, discipleship, fruit and repair, community and counsel—without assigning a number. Keep model comparisons as source comparison. Produce no spiritual total, marriage verdict, or inference about salvation.
