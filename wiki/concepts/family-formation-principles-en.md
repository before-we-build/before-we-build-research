# Family Formation Principles

English · [[family-formation-principles-ru|Русский]] · [[family-formation-principles-uk|Українська]]

<!-- section:in-90-seconds -->
## In 90 seconds

This page belongs to the Christian application, not to the universal core. In Christian family discernment, shared faith, truthful conduct, fidelity, responsibility, reciprocity, repentance, repair, consent, and safety come before typological interpretations. A typology may suggest a question; it cannot decide whether two people should marry.

<!-- section:scope -->
## Definition and scope

The application interprets the universal value-moral foundation through Christ, Scripture, observable fruit, church community, and mature counsel. It helps people examine a possible family commitment without turning attraction, a profile, pastoral authority, or AI output into a verdict.

**Biblical grounding:** Matthew 7:16–25; Galatians 5:22–23; Colossians 3:12–14; James 1:5.

<!-- section:foundational-priorities -->
## Foundational priorities

The first questions concern professed commitments and observable conduct over time: truthfulness, fidelity, responsibility, respect for boundaries, freedom of consent, willingness to repair harm, and the absence of coercion or abuse. Church participation or correct vocabulary is not a substitute for this evidence.

Safety is a gate. Violence, coercive control, exploitation, or inability to refuse freely must not be reframed as a communication mismatch or a difficult type combination.

**Biblical grounding:** Matthew 7:16–20; Ephesians 4:25–32; Philippians 2:3–4; James 3:13–18.

<!-- section:four-level-use -->
## How the four levels are used

After foundational issues are made explicit, the other levels organize conversation rather than rank the pair:

- the **strategic** level asks about temporal and existential direction;
- the **operational** level asks how shared action and responsibility are organized;
- the **tactical** level asks how information is modeled and exchanged.

Temporistics, Psychosophy, and Socionics are separate heuristic models of proposed latent processes. Their interpretations are research hypotheses, not facts about a person.

<!-- section:evidence-over-profiles -->
## Evidence before profiles

Repeated behavior, accountable decisions, repair after conflict, and testimony considered with appropriate caution matter more than a type label. An apparent pattern may also arise from stress, trauma, learned skill, doctrine, culture, material constraints, power imbalance, or the immediate situation.

<!-- section:limits-and-safety -->
## What must not be concluded

No type or combination establishes faith, character, holiness, guilt, innocence, readiness for marriage, compatibility in every context, or God’s will for a particular relationship. No current BWB percentage, weight, formula, or automatic pair verdict exists. AI may summarize material and propose questions, but it may not predict either person.

<!-- section:conversation-questions -->
## Questions for conversation

- Which Christian commitments govern this decision, and how are they visible in conduct?
- Can each person disagree, pause, set a boundary, and seek help freely?
- What happens after a promise is broken or a conflict causes harm?
- Where do direction, division of responsibility, or information exchange require an explicit agreement?
- Which interpretation would change if the context changed?

<!-- section:researcher-route -->
## For a researcher

Evaluate theological interpretation, observable conduct, pastoral usefulness, psychological measurement, and safety outcomes as separate questions. Record competing explanations and harms from false confidence; do not treat engagement or self-recognition as validation.
