# Four-Level Compatibility Architecture

English · [[four-level-compatibility-architecture-ru|Русский]] · [[four-level-compatibility-architecture-uk|Українська]]

<!-- section:in-90-seconds -->
## In 90 seconds

When two people disagree about a shared decision, it helps to ask what the disagreement concerns. What is acceptable? The future? The division of work? Or how each person understood the situation?

Before We Build calls these four groups of questions levels of compatibility. Values, commitments, and safety form the foundation. The project is studying whether typologies might help make sense of the other three levels. Here, a type is a hypothesis about a way of perceiving and organizing experience. It is not a personality type. Read more about [[typology-reconceptualization-en|the reinterpretation of typologies]].

<!-- section:definition-and-scope -->
## How the map works

| Level | What we consider | Core question |
|---|---|---|
| Value-moral foundation | worldview, commitments, conduct, consent, and safety; values, obligations, responsibility, reciprocity, and relationship repair | Which commitments and boundaries should guide what we do together? |
| Strategic | organizing the experience of time and the meaning of one’s life; the proposed model is Temporistics | Where are we going, and how do we connect past, present, and future? |
| Operational | analysis and synthesis in action; the proposed model is Psychosophy | How do we make decisions and act together? |
| Tactical | using information to form a picture of a situation; the proposed model is Socionics | What do we notice and how do we make sense of it? How do we share and revise our understanding? |

The first level sets out the moral commitments and boundaries. The project uses typological models only at the other three levels, to investigate possible internal processes.

<!-- section:shared-example -->
## Example: deciding to move

Two people want to move to another city. One helps an elderly relative. The question at the value-moral level is how to keep helping and which commitments must be honoured.

The strategic question is how they picture their future after the move. Who will look for housing, check the costs, and make decisions? Those are operational questions. The tactical question is how they tell each other about options and risks, spot mistakes, and clarify information.

<!-- section:observations -->
## What can be observed

Observe people’s actions and the outcomes. Listen to what they say about their commitments and plans. Notice how they make decisions, divide tasks, and share information. Grouping these observations by level helps organize the inquiry. It does not show that life itself is divided in exactly this way.

<!-- section:hypotheses -->
## What still needs testing

Considering possible causes of disagreement separately may help people ask more precise questions. We do not yet know whether the typologies accurately describe the proposed processes or can predict outcomes when two people interact.

<!-- section:alternatives -->
## What affects several levels at once

Law, money, culture, age, health, trauma, attachment, skills, and unequal power may matter at any level. Consider how these factors might affect the situation without attributing their effects to a person’s type.

<!-- section:non-inferences -->
## Limits of the map

The project does not prove that compatibility has exactly four natural layers. Agreement about the future does not guarantee agreement about work or values. Getting along at other levels cannot justify coercion or remove a threat to safety.

<!-- section:conversation-questions -->
## Questions for conversation

- Which of the four questions are we answering now?
- Are we using one word for several different disagreements?
- What circumstances change our answer?
- Are we asking anyone to sacrifice safety for the shared undertaking?

<!-- section:researcher-route -->
## How to test whether it helps

Compare this map with simpler explanations. State in advance the outcomes you want to predict and the observable indicators for each level. Then test whether indicators from different levels predict different outcomes and whether the map tells you anything the simpler models miss.
