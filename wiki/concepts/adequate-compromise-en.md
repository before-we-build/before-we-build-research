# Adequate Compromise

**Adequate compromise** is a BWB working term for a voluntary and feasible agreement that changes the form of joint action without violating essential duty, dignity, consent, or safety. It is not a canonical typology term or a way to calculate compatibility.

<!-- section:1-boundaries-before-form -->
## 1. Boundaries before form

Ordinary compromise is meaningful only after five checks:

| Check | Question | If the answer is no |
|---|---|---|
| Truth and commitments | Is there an option without sustained deception or betrayal of an essential duty? | acknowledge a substantive conflict |
| Consent and safety | Can each person refuse freely without threat, violence, or punishment? | stop ordinary negotiation and seek safety |
| Structure of shared life | Can the final requirements coexist in one undertaking? | acknowledge decision incompatibility |
| Reciprocity | Are cost, responsibility, and review rights shared? | examine exploitation or capitulation |
| Feasibility | Is there demonstrated capacity to perform and repair a breach? | do not rely on promises alone |

Typological interpretation cannot override any of these boundaries.

<!-- section:2-three-decision-zones -->
## 2. Three decision zones

| Zone | Observable situation | Approach |
|---|---|---|
| **Green** | foundation and goal agree; method differs | roles, schedule, budget, alternation, explicit communication format |
| **Yellow** | high cost, uncertainty, or effect on long-term direction | stages, reversibility, trial period, outside counsel, review date |
| **Red** | requirements are mutually exclusive, or coercion or danger is present | boundary, refusal, distance, or ending the shared project; not an arithmetic middle |

A red zone does not necessarily mean one person is worse. It means no freely acceptable shared decision currently exists.

<!-- section:3-four-level-check -->
## 3. Four-level check

- **Foundation:** which duty, consent, dignity, or safety condition is involved?
- **Strategic:** how does the option affect shared temporal and life direction?
- **Operational:** who decides, bears the cost, acts, and revises the agreement?
- **Tactical:** what information and explanatory form does each person need?

Context—health, money, law, culture, power, and dependants—crosses every question.

<!-- section:4-observations-instead-of-a-typological-verdict -->
## 4. Observations instead of a typological verdict

Record each proposal, ability to refuse, cost distribution, performance over time, breaches, and repair. A type pattern may suggest a conversational format; it does not establish an inner “structure,” vulnerability, or impossibility of a union.

Rival explanations include missing skill, an unclear agreement, fatigue, trauma, constrained resources, unequal power, or incorrect facts. Test them before a typological explanation.

<!-- section:5-illustrative-example -->
## 5. Illustrative example

For relocation, one person requests more scenarios while the other wants a decision before an offer expires. An adequate form might be a short list of required facts, a named research owner, and a review date. But if the move abandons a dependant or consent is obtained by threat, the problem is not decision style and cannot be repaired by a typological format.

<!-- section:6-conversation-questions -->
## 6. Conversation questions

- What is each person actually protecting?
- Which part is negotiable, and which is a duty or boundary?
- Who bears the cost and may reopen the question?
- How will we know that the agreement works?
- Which fact or repeated outcome would require revision?
