# Temporal Aspects in Temporistics

<!-- section:overview -->
## Overview

Temporistics uses four aspects representing different orientations in time.

<!-- section:the-four-aspects -->
## The Four Aspects

| Aspect | Description | Thinking process | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P** (Past) | Past orientation | Causal reasoning (causal analysis) | “Why did this happen?”: memory, origins, precedents, causal links |
| **N** (Present / Now) | Present orientation | Situational-experiential reasoning (contact with the moment) | “What is happening now and where is my place?”: involvement, direct conditions |
| **F** (Future) | Future orientation | Scenario-probabilistic reasoning (trajectory modeling) | “Where are we going?”: probabilistic scenarios, planning, course revision |
| **E** (Eternity) | Eternity orientation | Meaning-making and sense-making (teleological analysis) | “Why and to what end?”: overarching meaning, purpose, enduring values |

<!-- section:past-vs-eternity-distinction -->
## Distinguishing Past and Eternity: Causality vs Meaning-Making

It is critically important not to conflate retrospective causal analysis (Past) with meaning-making processes (Eternity):

| Criterion | Past ($P$) — Causal Reasoning | Eternity ($E$) — Meaning-Making / Sense-Making |
|---|---|---|
| **Core question** | “Why?” / “Where did this come from?” | “To what end?” / “Why is this significant?” |
| **Link type** | Causal (causes, chronology, conditions, consequences) | Teleological and axiological (overarching meaning, values) |
| **Focus of operation** | Memory, facts, drawing lessons from past successes and errors | Principles, purpose, relating the immediate situation to broader meaning |
| **Verification focus** | Factual accuracy: “Did $X$ actually cause $Y$?” | Meaning coherence: “Does this carry enduring significance?” |
| **Potential imbalance** | Fixation on causes and past errors without a clear “why keep going” | Formulating grand missions and meanings disconnected from historical experience and causes |

Motivational exploratory drive (*Novelty Seeking* in Cloninger's TCI, *BAS* in Gray's RST) or error sensitivity (*Harm Avoidance* / *BIS*) map in BWB to *positional modes of resource allocation* (positions 1 and 3), rather than being monopolized by any single temporal aspect (see [[temporistics-cognitive-resource-routing-creation-model-en]]).

<!-- section:eternity-oriented-wishes -->
## Eternity-Oriented Wishes

Use Eternity-oriented wishes when the intent is to support a person's relationship with meaning, meaning-making processes, purpose, values, or existential continuity. These formulations should not imply that Eternity position determines a person's social rank or human worth.

Good wishes for the Eternity aspect name the person's right to meaning-making and meaning without forcing a fixed doctrine:

| Theme | Example wish |
|-------|--------------|
| Meaning | "May your life feel connected to something that is truly meaningful to you." |
| Integrity | "May your values stay alive in your everyday choices." |
| Continuity | "May difficult experiences find a place in a larger, kinder story." |
| Openness | "May you meet ideas, teachers, and traditions that expand rather than imprison you." |
| Humility | "May your worldview stay strong enough to guide you and flexible enough to learn." |

Avoid caste-like or rank-like wishes such as "become higher," "rise above ordinary people," or "find your superior mission." Before We Build treats Eternity as a temporal/existential process (meaning-making), not as a model of human value.
