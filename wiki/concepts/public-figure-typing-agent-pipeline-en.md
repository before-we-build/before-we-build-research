# A Human-Led Evidence Workflow for Public-Figure Hypotheses

<!-- section:status -->
## Status

This is a research process for criticizing typological hypotheses, not a way to establish a “true type.” It separates source, observation, interpretation, and alternative. “Insufficient evidence” can and often should be the outcome.

Despite the page's historical slug, human researchers make the decisions. AI may serve only as a weak assistant: summarize already selected material, organize a table, and suggest questions. It does not type or predict a person, assign a code or confidence, or make a moral or pastoral judgment.

<!-- section:main-flow -->
## Main flow

```text
human source selection
→ table of exact observations
→ independent blind coding
→ separate reviews for three systems
→ role and alternative critique
→ safety review
→ human arbitration or no conclusion
```

One participant should not simultaneously select convenient sources, code traces, and defend a preferred type.

<!-- section:sources-and-limitations -->
## Sources and limitations

| Material | What it may provide | Main risk |
|---|---|---|
| official biography | dates, roles, institutional trajectory | editing and role-pattern confounding |
| full interview | explanations of choice, conflict, and error | self-presentation, editing, interviewer prompts |
| full sermon or lecture | argument structure and recurring themes | genre, theological school, prepared text |
| book or article | conceptual architecture over time | editor and subject matter |
| questionnaire or self-report | participant's own words with consent | wording and self-perception effects |
| short clip or summary | a lead to the full source | insufficient for typological inference |

The corpus should span years, genres, and roles. Each quotation is stored with URL, date, context, and a note on what it actually supports.

<!-- section:researcher-roles -->
## Researcher roles

| Role | Task | Does not do |
|---|---|---|
| method reviewer | selects applicable methods and limits | preselect a code |
| source curator | checks provenance and completeness | turn a topic directly into a trace |
| blind coders | describe speech and action without name or office | see the initial hypothesis |
| three system reviewers | separately consider Socionics, Psychosophy, and Temporistics | mix levels or moral conclusions |
| critic | searches for role overfit, genre effects, and equal alternatives | invent a more attractive code |
| safety reviewer | checks dignity, privacy, and harmful-use boundaries | assess spirituality |
| arbiter | records disagreement and decides whether evidence suffices | inflate confidence for closure |

<!-- section:evidence-row-format -->
## Evidence-row format

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| source | exact link, date, genre, authorship or editing |
| excerpt | short quotation or verifiable episode description |
| observation | what was said or done without typological terms |
| context | role, audience, crisis, preparedness |
| possible interpretations | at least one typological and one non-typological account |
| disconfirming evidence | what would weaken the interpretation |

An AI summary never replaces a primary-source row and must be checked by a person.

<!-- section:qualitative-confidence-states -->
## Qualitative confidence states

- **insufficient evidence** — the corpus or coder agreement cannot sustain a code;
- **tentative hypothesis** — a recurring trace exists, but role and alternatives explain it comparably well;
- **conditionally supported hypothesis** — independent coders and multiple genres converge, and key alternatives have been tested.

These states are not probabilities or ratings of a person. Without a voluntary interview, conclusions about a public figure remain limited regardless of text volume.

<!-- section:arbitration -->
## Arbitration

The arbiter records a primary candidate only together with alternatives, contrary evidence, and next differentiating questions. If reviewers substantially disagree, no code is selected. Office, one topic, eloquence, or popularity alone does not increase confidence.

<!-- section:use-limits -->
## Use limits

The result must not support spiritual or moral assessment, staffing, trust decisions, reputational ranking, marriage conclusions, or behavioural prediction. Public status does not remove dignity, context, or the right not to be reduced to a model.
