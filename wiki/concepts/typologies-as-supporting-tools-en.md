# Typologies as Supporting Tools

English · [[typologies-as-supporting-tools-ru|Русский]] · [[typologies-as-supporting-tools-uk|Українська]]

<!-- section:in-90-seconds -->
## In 90 seconds

In the Christian application, typologies are optional question-generating tools. They may help name recurring differences in temporal direction, joint action, or information exchange. They are not spiritual diagnoses, portraits of a whole person, moral rankings, destiny, or evidence that a relationship is safe.

<!-- section:definition-and-scope -->
## Definition and scope

A **type** is a model hypothesis or compact description of a proposed pattern, not an observable object or a kind of human being. A **latent process** is inferred from indicators and remains a research hypothesis, not an established causal mechanism. The application must keep both terms subordinate to direct observation, Scripture-grounded discernment, consent, safety, and accountable counsel.

<!-- section:possible-use -->
## What typologies may help with

Used cautiously, a model can suggest questions about a recurring disagreement, expose assumptions that have not been discussed, or offer vocabulary for comparing observations across situations. Its value lies in the quality of the conversation and subsequent checking, not in how recognizable a profile feels.

<!-- section:system-mappings -->
## The three non-equivalent models

Before We Build currently studies three separate mappings:

- **Temporistics** → proposed latent processes of abduction, induction, and deduction in temporal or existential experience;
- **Psychosophy** → proposed latent processes of synthesis and analysis in action;
- **Socionics** → proposed latent processes of information modeling.

The systems are modeled separately. This does not establish that they are empirically valid or statistically independent.

<!-- section:observation-and-hypothesis -->
## Observation and hypothesis

“They postponed the same decision three times” is an observation if the record supports it. “This reflects a Temporistics position” is an interpretation. It becomes more credible only if preregistered indicators recur across contexts, rival explanations are tested, and independent assessment produces similar results.

<!-- section:alternative-explanations -->
## Alternative explanations

The same pattern may arise from skill, stress, trauma, role expectations, doctrine, language, culture, health, resources, incentives, power, or the immediate situation. The model must compete with these explanations rather than absorb them into a type portrait.

<!-- section:non-inferences -->
## What typologies cannot establish

No type establishes faith, dignity, character, salvation, sanctification, guilt, innocence, compatibility in every context, readiness for marriage, abuse, or God’s will. No combination guarantees ease or failure. Individual test output is not a pair-compatibility score.

<!-- section:christian-safeguards -->
## Christian safeguards

Observable fruit, truthful conduct, repentance, responsibility, reciprocity, repair, free consent, and safety take priority. Typological language must never excuse sin, conceal coercion, pressure a person to continue a relationship, or replace pastoral, clinical, legal, or safeguarding help.

**Biblical grounding:** Matthew 7:16–20; 1 Corinthians 8:1; 1 Thessalonians 5:21; James 3:13–18.

<!-- section:conversation-questions -->
## Questions for conversation

- What did we directly observe, and what did we infer?
- Which other explanation fits the same evidence?
- What evidence would count against the typological interpretation?
- Does the label increase mutual understanding, or reduce curiosity about the person?
- Is there any safety issue that must be handled outside typology?

<!-- section:researcher-route -->
## For a researcher

Define the construct and indicators before collecting outcomes. Test reliability, convergent and discriminant validity, incremental value over simpler explanations, context sensitivity, and harms from misclassification. Keep measurement of one person separate from any future pair-outcome model.
