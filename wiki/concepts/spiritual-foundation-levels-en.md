# Spiritual Foundation and Pair Direction

This page is a Christian specialization of the Before We Build value-moral foundation. It helps ask before Whom and for what purpose a shared life is built. It is not a spiritual typology, maturity scale, judgement of salvation, or substitute for Scripture, church, pastoral counsel, and personal responsibility.

<!-- section:1-biblical-frame -->
## 1. Biblical frame

- **Matthew 7:24–27:** a house's foundation is tested by doing Christ's words, not by outward form.
- **Luke 14:28–30:** serious building requires an honest account of cost.
- **Proverbs 24:3–4; 15:22:** wisdom, knowledge, and counsel participate in building a house.
- **Psalm 127:1:** a human project is not self-sufficient.
- **1 Corinthians 12:13–22:** differences in gifts and functions do not diminish the worth of members of the body.

These texts define normative questions for the Christian application. They are not psychometric indicators.

<!-- section:2-distinctions-to-preserve -->
## 2. Distinctions to preserve

| Layer | What is considered | What cannot be inferred from it |
|---|---|---|
| Spiritual foundation | professed centre, obedience to Christ, fruit, repentance, conscience, church accountability | salvation, election, or human worth |
| Typological hypothesis | a possible information, action, or temporal-experience pattern | faith, moral maturity, or God's will about marriage |
| Observable conduct | decisions, responsibility, harm, repair, longitudinal consistency | the whole heart or final spiritual status |

It is safer to speak about **observable spiritual direction and trajectory** than to assign a level or rank to a person.

<!-- section:3-four-areas-of-inquiry -->
## 3. Four areas of inquiry

| Area | Question | Possible observations | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Professed foundation | Whom or what does the person recognize as the highest guide? | their words and choices when desire conflicts with conviction | words may reflect cultural form or adaptation |
| Discipleship | Is the person open to Scripture, correction, and growth? | willingness to learn, admit error, and change practice | one agreement does not establish stability |
| Fruit and repair | What recurs in treatment of others and after harm? | love, self-control, truthfulness, responsibility, repentance, restitution | an observer lacks full context and motive |
| Community and counsel | Is there voluntary accountability to a mature community? | long relationships, checkable reputation, reception of counsel | an authority can err or abuse power |

<!-- section:4-possible-pair-dynamics -->
## 4. Possible pair dynamics

**Shared direction:** both visibly seek to submit shared life to Christ, Scripture, repentance, and mature counsel. This does not promise absence of conflict.

**Different maturity within shared direction:** differences in experience may be ordinary but require protection against control, rescuing, and turning marriage into a mentoring project.

**Unclear foundation:** Christian language or cultural proximity is present, but stable personal direction is not yet visible. Preserve uncertainty rather than close it with a desired conclusion.

**Different direction:** the two name different highest foundations. Easy communication or practical complementarity does not remove a real conflict of commitments.

These descriptions are a question map, not four pair types.

<!-- section:5-rival-explanations-and-risks -->
## 5. Rival explanations and risks

New faith, trauma, illness, conflict with an unsafe community, cultural language, lack of mentoring, or fear may affect observations. Conversely, religious activity may coexist with control, deception, and failure to repair. Time, several sources, and attention to power are needed.

Spiritual language must not be used to force someone into a relationship, keep them in danger, prevent help-seeking, or compel submission to a partner's private interpretation.

<!-- section:6-questions-before-a-serious-decision -->
## 6. Questions before a serious decision

1. How does each person understand Christ's lordship in concrete decisions?
2. What happens when Scripture, conscience, or duty constrains desire?
3. How does the person acknowledge harm, repent, and rebuild trust?
4. Can each seek counsel independently of the partner?
5. Are consent, safety, and a right to pause the decision present?
6. Which observations support our reading and which contradict it?
