# Temporal Interpersonal Emotion Systems: The "TIES" That Form Relationships

**Author:** Emily A. Butler  
**Journal:** *Personality and Social Psychology Review*, 2011, 15(4), pp. 367–393  
**DOI/URL:** [https://doi.org/10.1177/1088868311411164](https://doi.org/10.1177/1088868311411164)  
**Study Type:** Theoretical synthesis and methodological framework for dynamical dyadic systems  

---

## Abstract & Overview

Interpersonal relationships are fundamentally characterized by emotional dynamics that unfold across time. This paper introduces the Temporal Interpersonal Emotion Systems (TIES) framework, conceptualizing interpersonal emotional dynamics as emergent properties of an interconnected, non-linear dynamic system. Rather than viewing emotion as an individual state or compatibility as static trait similarity, TIES models experiential, behavioral, and physiological components of emotion as continuously coupled both within individuals and between partners.

## Key Theoretical Dimensions & Typology of Linkage

1. **Interpersonal Emotional Linkage (Emotional Coupling):** The statistical and dynamic coupling between partners' emotional states across time. Linkage can take multiple functional forms:
   - *In-phase synchrony / covariation:* Concurrent rise and fall of emotional parameters.
   - *Anti-phase / complementary oscillation:* One partner dampening while the other escalates.
   - *Attractor states and stability:* The baseline equilibrium states to which a dyad returns following emotional disturbance.
   - *Perturbation and dampening vs. amplification:* How external shocks or internal conflicts propagate through the dyadic feedback loop.
2. **Coupling Is Not Inherently Benevolent:** High interpersonal emotional linkage does not automatically equal healthy communication or compatibility. Severe linkage often reflects emotional contagion of distress, conflict escalation, or mutual entrapment in destructive attractor states. Conversely, healthy coordination often requires emotional buffering and asymmetric dampening rather than identical matching.
3. **Multi-Channel Organization:** Emotional dynamics operate simultaneously across subjective experience, expressive behavior (facial, vocal, kinesic), and autonomic physiology (HRV, skin conductance), with potential cross-channel and cross-partner latencies.

## Relevance to Before We Build (BWB)

- **Dynamic Compatibility Baseline:** Butler (2011) provides the mathematical and conceptual foundation for BWB's definition of compatibility: compatibility is not a static distance metric between two psychometric profiles, but the ongoing capacity of a dyad to maintain adaptive, non-destructive attractor dynamics and recover from perturbations.
- **Cross-Level Interdependence:** Validates the BWB architecture where tactical communication (Socionics) and operational action (Psychosophy) modulate shared emotional and energetic states across time.
- **Epistemic Caution:** Emphasizes that synchrony or linkage cannot be treated as a positive compatibility score. Dyads can synchronize into mutual panic, trauma reenactment, or abusive dynamics.
