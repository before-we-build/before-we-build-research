# Merged Minds: Generalized Shared Reality in Dyadic Relationships

**Authors:** Maya Rossignac-Milon, Niall Bolger, Katherine S. Zee, Erica J. Boothby, E. Tory Higgins  
**Journal:** *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology (JPSP)*, 2021, 120(4), pp. 882–911  
**DOI/URL:** [https://doi.org/10.1037/pspi0000266](https://doi.org/10.1037/pspi0000266)  
**Study Type:** Empirical multi-method dyadic research program (9 studies)  

---

## Abstract & Overview

How do close partners come to experience that they perceive the world through the same lens? Extending Shared Reality Theory from ephemeral situational commonality to enduring interpersonal relationships, the authors investigate *generalized shared reality*—the subjective feeling that two minds perceive, interpret, and understand reality in the same way. Across 9 laboratory, observational, and field studies involving both established romantic couples and unacquainted dyads, the authors developed and validated a generalized shared reality measure and tested its psychological functions.

## Key Empirical Findings

1. **Construct Independence:** Generalized shared reality is empirically distinct from general relationship satisfaction, emotional closeness, perceived similarity, and perceived partner responsiveness, though positively correlated with them.
2. **Predictive Validity of Closeness:** The subjective experience of shared reality predicted relationship commitment, certainty, and felt security above and beyond standard relationship measures.
3. **Compensatory Dynamics Under Threat:** When partners' shared reality was experimentally threatened (by highlighting differences in perception or interpretation of stimuli), couples with strong baseline shared reality exhibited immediate compensatory behaviors—actively communicating and seeking to restore epistemic alignment.
4. **Dyadic Emergence in Strangers:** Even among previously unacquainted strangers interacting for the first time, dyads that achieved high turn-by-turn conversational coordination rapidly reported an emergent sense of generalized shared reality.

## Relevance to Before We Build (BWB)

- **Tactical Level (Level 4 Grounding):** Provides the definitive psychological counterpart to BWB's tactical layer (reconstructed Socionics). It proves that communication is primarily an epistemic coordination mechanism—partners do not merely exchange facts, but actively build and defend a common model of reality.
- **Dynamic Construction vs. Static Matching:** Demonstrates that shared reality is forged and sustained through reciprocal interaction and conversational repair, not determined solely by pre-existing personality traits.
- **Epistemic Caution:** The authors explicitly note that *perceived* shared reality is subjective epistemic commonality; it does not guarantee that the partners' joint model of reality is factually correct, nor does it prove that human personality consists of 16 discrete information types.
