# Acute Stress Impacts Executive-Social Function: Evidence From Prefrontal Activation and fNIRS-Based Hyperscanning

**Authors:** Zhihua Guo, Yue Gong, Liu Yang, Y. Li, R. Qiu, Xia Zhu  
**Journal:** *Brain and Behavior*, January 2026, e70231  
**DOI/URL:** [https://doi.org/10.1002/brb3.70231](https://doi.org/10.1002/brb3.70231) (PubMed: 39823412)  
**Study Type:** Empirical dyadic neuroimaging / fNIRS hyperscanning  

---

## Abstract & Study Overview

Acute stress alters both cognitive control (executive function) and social decision-making, yet these domains have historically been examined in isolation using single-participant designs. This study utilized dual-brain functional near-infrared spectroscopy (fNIRS hyperscanning) to record real-time hemodynamic activity across prefrontal cortical regions in dyads engaged in cooperative cognitive problem-solving under acute stress compared to non-stress baseline conditions.

## Key Findings

1. **Executive–Social Function Coupling Hypothesis:** The authors formulate an integrative heuristic hypothesis stating that under acute stress, prefrontal networks mediating executive control (e.g., dlPFC, working memory, attention shifting) and social cognition (e.g., IFG, perspective-taking, communication alignment) functionally couple into a unified adaptive system.
2. **Prefrontal Hemodynamic Shifts:** Acute stress elicited elevated oxygenated hemoglobin (HbO) in the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (dlPFC) and inferior frontal gyrus (IFG), signaling heightened metabolic demand during joint decision-making under threat/pressure.
3. **Inter-Brain Synchrony (IBS):** Cooperative dyads showed increased prefrontal neural synchrony during successful task coordination. However, when individual stress loads exceeded cognitive capacity, neural synchrony degraded, accompanied by behavioral desynchronization and communication breakdown.
4. **Dyadic Buffering vs. Overload:** Dyads that maintained communicative turn-taking and mutual pacing demonstrated a neural buffering effect against acute stress, whereas rigid or unilateral decision-making exacerbated cognitive fatigue and conflict.

## Relevance to Before We Build (BWB)

- **Tactical Level (Reality Modeling & Conversational Repair):** Provides direct neuroimaging evidence that real-time social coordination is an active, turn-by-turn co-regulation process, mirroring BWB's interpretation of tactical communication as mutual model updating rather than static trait matching.
- **Operational Level (Action & Resource Coordination under Pressure):** Confirms that acute stress demands flexible role reallocation; rigidity in execution leads to dyadic breakdown.
- **Epistemic Caution:** The study measures laboratory task hemodynamic responses in dyads; it does NOT prove any typological personality model, Model A position, or innate typology.
