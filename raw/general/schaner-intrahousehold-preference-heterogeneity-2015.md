# Do Opposites Detract? Intrahousehold Preference Heterogeneity and Inefficient Strategic Savings

**Author:** Simone Schaner  
**Journal:** *American Economic Journal: Applied Economics*, 2015, 7(2), pp. 135–174  
**DOI/URL:** [https://doi.org/10.1257/app.20130271](https://doi.org/10.1257/app.20130271)  
**Study Type:** Randomized field experiment on intrahousehold decision-making and intertemporal preferences  

---

## Abstract & Overview

Standard unitary household models assume that married couples act as single optimizing economic agents. This study tests intrahousehold efficiency by examining how heterogeneity in spouses' intertemporal preferences (time discount rates) affects joint and individual savings choices. Through a randomized field experiment in rural Kenya involving real bank accounts with randomly assigned individual and joint interest rates, the author directly observes household financial decision-making under preference alignment versus preference conflict.

## Key Empirical Findings

1. **Strategic Inefficiency from Preference Mismatch:** Couples with large differences in time preference (one partner patient/forward-looking, the other impatient/immediate-oriented) made systematically inefficient savings decisions, often sacrificing substantial interest income on joint accounts to maintain individual financial control.
2. **Mistrust and Strategic Hiding:** Preference heterogeneity generated strategic friction: impatient spouses sought immediate liquidity, while patient spouses attempted to lock away funds, leading to costly hiding behavior and suboptimal resource allocation.
3. **Well-Matched Couples Flourish:** Spouses who shared similar intertemporal preferences responded strongly to relative interest rate incentives and maximized collective financial returns, demonstrating efficient cooperative bargaining.

## Relevance to Before We Build (BWB)

- **Strategic Level (Level 2 Grounding):** Provides rigorous, real-world field-experimental evidence that differences in temporal horizons and time perspectives directly impact the survival, welfare, and operational viability of a shared household.
- **Empirical Refinement of Matching:** Refines the simplistic claim that "no trait matching matters": while static personality inventories fail to predict emotional satisfaction (Joel et al., 2020), concrete intertemporal preference alignment (temporal horizons) does matter for long-term material coordination.
- **Epistemic Caution:** The study measures elicited discount factors in monetary experiments; it does not measure existential time orientations (Past/Present/Future/Eternity) or validate Temporistics as a typology of personality. Economic bargaining and institutional gender dynamics represent major rival explanations.
