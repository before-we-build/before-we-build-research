# Machine Learning Uncovers the Most Robust Self-Report Predictors of Relationship Quality Across 43 Longitudinal Couples Studies

**Authors:** Samantha Joel, Paul W. Eastwick, Colleen J. Allison, ... Eli J. Finkel  
**Journal:** *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences (PNAS)*, August 2020, 117 (32) 19061-19071  
**DOI/URL:** [https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1917036117](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1917036117)  
**Study Type:** Large-scale empirical meta-dataset / Machine learning benchmark (Random Forests)  

---

## Abstract & Study Overview

What predicts how happy people are with their romantic relationships, and does partner combination matter? Using Random Forest machine learning algorithms across 43 longitudinal datasets involving 11,196 couples and 2,278 self-report variables, the authors systematically compared the predictive power of individual-difference variables versus relationship-specific variables in predicting relationship quality at baseline and over time.

## Key Findings

1. **Relationship-Specific Dynamics Dominate:** Relationship-specific variables (perceived partner commitment, appreciation, sexual satisfaction, perceived partner responsiveness, conflict frequency) accounted for up to ~45% of variance in relationship satisfaction at baseline.
2. **Individual Differences Have Limited Power:** Individual-difference variables (e.g., Big Five traits, life satisfaction, depression, attachment anxiety/avoidance) explained only ~21% of variance for the individual reporting them, and virtually 0% of their partner's satisfaction.
3. **Failure of Dyadic Matching / Trait Compatibility:** Combinations or interactions between partners' individual traits (the mathematical premise of questionnaire-based dating compatibility algorithms) explained negligible (effectively zero) incremental variance beyond individual main effects. A person's traits do not dictate who they will match with.
4. **Unpredictability of Trajectory Changes:** While cross-sectional relationship quality is substantially predictable from relationship-specific factors, *changes* in relationship satisfaction over longitudinal follow-ups were largely unpredictable from baseline self-reports alone.

## Relevance to Before We Build (BWB)

- **Rejection of Flat Compatibility Scores:** Joel et al. (2020) provides the most comprehensive empirical benchmark refuting static trait-matching algorithms and flat compatibility scores.
- **Support for Multi-Level Process Architecture:** The findings prove that compatibility is not an inherent trait combination, but an emergent property of relationship processes (commitment, perceived responsiveness, conflict navigation).
- **Epistemic Humility:** Individual profiles cannot forecast relationship longevity; conversational tools must help couples navigate differences rather than deliver fate verdicts.
