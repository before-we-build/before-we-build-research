# Transactive Goal Dynamics

**Authors:** Grainne M. Fitzsimons, Eli J. Finkel, Michelle R. vanDellen  
**Journal:** *Psychological Review*, 2015, 122(4), pp. 648–673  
**DOI/URL:** [https://doi.org/10.1037/a0039654](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0039654)  
**Study Type:** Theoretical synthesis and conceptual model of dyadic self-regulation  

---

## Abstract & Overview

Traditional psychological models treat goal pursuit and self-regulation as intrapsychic processes occurring within an individual mind. Transactive Goal Dynamics (TGD) theory proposes that when individuals become interdependent, their individual self-regulatory systems merge into a coupled, transactive goal dynamic system. Within this system, partners are no longer independent actors; instead, each partner's goals, pursuits, and outcomes mutually modulate and constrain the other's, creating emergent dyadic goal pursuit.

## Core Theoretical Architecture

1. **System Structure:**
   - *Self-oriented goals:* Goals held by Person A for Person A.
   - *Partner-oriented goals:* Goals held by Person A for Person B.
   - *System-oriented (shared) goals:* Goals held by the dyad as a collective entity.
2. **Key Transactive Mechanisms:**
   - *Goal Allocation:* The division of self-regulatory labor across partners, enabling efficiency and specialization.
   - *Regulatory Interdependence:* One partner's success or failure in goal execution directly impacts the cognitive and volitional resources available to the other partner.
   - *Coordination Traps & Asymmetries:* Inefficient goal allocation or unilateral burdening can lead to systemic failure, resentment, and depletion, even when individual motivation is high.
3. **Efficiency vs. Agency:** High transactive efficiency can reduce individual cognitive load, but risks personal disempowerment if one partner becomes dependent on the other for basic self-regulation.

## Relevance to Before We Build (BWB)

- **Operational Level (Level 3 Grounding):** Provides the foundational academic framework for BWB's operational layer (reconstructed Psychosophy). BWB interprets Psychosophy not as fixed soul traits, but as heuristic patterns of how agency, volition, and practical tasks are organized and negotiated in shared action.
- **Support for Multi-Level Independence:** TGD demonstrates that two individuals can have shared moral values (Level 1) and agree on long-term directions (Level 2), but suffer severe relational strain if their daily transactive goal pursuit (Level 3) is mismatched, uncoordinated, or exploitative.
- **Epistemic Caution:** TGD is an established psychological theory that explains goal coordination without requiring typological functions (Logic, Physics, Volition, Emotion). Typological labels must remain heuristic maps for dialogue rather than mandatory ontological categories.
