# Observation

Repository pages and agent instructions often reduced Socionics aspects to
themes such as comfort, efficiency, or emotion. Position labels such as
“strong,” “weak,” and “vulnerable” were then easily read as ability or deficit
scores. The source layer, the BWB reconstruction, observable traces, and a
possible natural predisposition were not consistently separated.

# Resolution

The canonical BWB account now treats an aspect as a revisable hypothesis about
a reality-difference class and a modeling operation. Model A positions are a
separate hypothesis about operation-use modes. Agents must preserve the chain
from shared reality through partial representation, inference, feedback, and
update; test both layers separately; and reject ability, brain-module,
innateness, exhaustive-channel, and deterministic relationship inferences.
