# Teams and friendship: source search record

Search date: 2026-09-06. Owner: delegated literature researcher.

## Question and scope

Which established empirical papers give Before We Build useful comparison variables for teamwork and friendship formation or maintenance? This is a purposeful seed selection for eight readable cards, not a systematic search, current consensus review, or claim to include every relevant paper. All eight selected publications are peer-reviewed. The review papers can themselves include dissertations or other unpublished primary results.

Included: identifiable publication and DOI; direct journal, PubMed, author, or institutional source; a measured relational/team outcome; enough accessible information to distinguish design, findings, and limits. Priority: meta-analyses for teams, followed by primary friendship studies that distinguish similarity, contact opportunities, and maintenance.

Excluded from this first batch: popular summaries as evidence, typology-community claims, duplicate summaries of the same paper, and papers without sufficient verified content. Search results about friendship genetics and mobile-phone network turnover were not selected: the present eight-card scope focuses on measured interaction and closeness. No assessment of all omitted literature was performed.

## Findings and research use

Team outcomes have established candidate predictors beyond any BWB type-pattern hypothesis: conflict, trust, measured Big Five traits, coordination of knowledge, task interdependence, and training. Friendship work separates independently measured similarity from perceived similarity, and distinguishes contact opportunities and continuing investment from elapsed acquaintance duration.

Suggested comparison variables: measured traits, experience, task requirements, previous performance, trust, actual reliability, conflict episodes, shared knowledge, contact time, leisure activity, perceived similarity, initial closeness, distance, and life transition. Suggested outcomes: separate objective team performance from satisfaction, and measure friendship closeness repeatedly. No new scale or compatibility score is authorized by these papers.

## Limitations, disagreements, and provenance handoff

- Years 2003–2019 describe this selected set, not the date limit of a systematic search. Updated syntheses are a next search priority.
- Most selected evidence is correlational. The controlled-training review provides a different kind of evidence, with heterogeneous intervention designs and settings.
- Do not infer that task disagreement is always harmful, that similarity is universally helpful or irrelevant, or that shared time guarantees friendship.
- Do not add sample counts across reviews: underlying studies may overlap.
- Small friendship cohorts and student settings restrict population coverage. Gender differences in Roberts and Dunbar must not become individual prescriptions.
- Team-level findings require separate testing for dyads; none establishes BWB's Socionics, Psychosophy, or Temporistics hypotheses.
- Provenance audit priorities: full-text verification of de Jong and Selfhout; Selfhout sample size; historical author-name metadata discrepancy (PubMed lists Selfhout, Utrecht's current record lists van Zalk). The card retains the publication's PubMed name, without treating repository metadata as a second study.
- Hall's publication-year discrepancy is online-first versus issue dating, not two separate studies.
