# Public-figure typology: ethics and legal review gate

**Status:** active risk-control document

**Last reviewed:** 2026-08-30

**Scope:** exploratory research records based on public material; not legal
advice and not permission to publish or commercialize a profile.

## Purpose

This document defines when Before We Build may retain an exploratory
public-source typing record and when work must stop for human ethics, privacy,
or legal review. It deliberately does not declare public-figure typing “legal,”
“safe,” or scientifically valid. Those conclusions depend on the jurisdiction,
purpose, data, publisher, audience, and consequences for the person concerned.

## Non-negotiable boundaries

- A public role does not eliminate privacy, dignity, accuracy, or data-protection
  interests.
- Publicly accessible information is not automatically free of purpose,
  licensing, retention, or profiling constraints.
- A typology code is a project hypothesis, not an observed property, diagnosis,
  moral judgement, fitness assessment, or prediction of conduct.
- No profile may be used for employment, insurance, credit, political targeting,
  pastoral authority, safety screening, or another consequential decision.
- No relationship or pair verdict may be inferred between people who have not
  provided relevant evidence and consent.
- Do not infer health, religion, sexuality, trauma, criminality, or another
  sensitive fact from style, appearance, occupation, or a typology model.
- Do not imitate the person's voice or likeness or generate statements presented
  as theirs.

## Why a disclaimer is not enough

Calling a profile “opinion” does not by itself resolve accuracy, defamation,
privacy, data-protection, research-ethics, or professional-ethics questions.
Likewise, saying “not a diagnosis” does not make a detailed psychological claim
well supported. The claim still needs traceable evidence, bounded wording,
serious alternative explanations, and a proportionate reason to publish it.

The American Psychiatric Association's Goldwater Rule formally governs its
psychiatrist members, not this repository. It is nevertheless a useful restraint:
the Association says psychiatrists may discuss issues in general but must not
offer a professional opinion about a public figure without examination and
authorization. The American Psychological Association's assessment standard
likewise requires sufficient information and techniques for evaluative claims.
Neither source is a shortcut that endorses non-clinical remote typing.

## Data-protection checkpoint

Automated evaluation of personal data can constitute profiling. The UK
Information Commissioner's Office states that data-protection duties can still
apply even when a process does not make a solely automated decision with legal
or similarly significant effects. Its guidance is currently being revised after
changes in UK law, so repository text must not freeze a jurisdiction-wide legal
conclusion.

Before collecting, transforming, publishing, or operationalizing an identifiable
person's data, a responsible owner must document:

1. jurisdiction and organizational role;
2. specific purpose and necessity;
3. data categories, sources, copyright/licensing status, and retention period;
4. lawful basis and transparency obligations where data-protection law applies;
5. whether automated profiling or a consequential decision is involved;
6. risks of inaccuracy, bias, harassment, reputational harm, and reuse;
7. correction, objection, removal, and human-review processes;
8. whether a DPIA, ethics review, or qualified legal advice is required.

If the owner cannot answer these questions, the profile remains private research
material and is not used in a product or public decision.

## Contract for an exploratory research record

Every retained record must:

- state that the person did not participate in a direct assessment unless they
  actually did;
- identify each public source precisely enough to verify it;
- separate observed words/actions from the author's interpretation;
- label every proposed type as a competing research hypothesis;
- include non-typological explanations and disconfirming evidence;
- record missing evidence and uncertainty;
- avoid compatibility rankings, “best partner” claims, or behavioural forecasts;
- link to this review gate;
- use neutral, non-stigmatizing language;
- have a named human reviewer before external publication.

A research record is methodologically useful only if another reviewer can trace
how an observation became a hypothesis and can reasonably disagree with that
step.

## Stop and escalate

Stop the analysis and seek the relevant human review when any of the following
is present:

- the person is private, a child, or otherwise vulnerable;
- the material is leaked, intimate, unlawfully obtained, or unexpectedly
  sensitive;
- the intended use could materially affect rights, access, reputation, safety,
  livelihood, or political participation;
- the profile would be monetized, advertised, or used for targeting;
- the author intends a clinical, legal, security, or fitness conclusion;
- a correction, objection, or removal request has been received;
- applicable law or platform terms are unclear.

## Publication checklist

- [ ] The purpose cannot be met with an anonymous or fictional example.
- [ ] Observations, quotations, and citations have been verified.
- [ ] Interpretation and competing hypotheses are visibly separate.
- [ ] No type is presented as a fact about the person.
- [ ] No moral worth, safety, destiny, or pair outcome is inferred from type.
- [ ] Sensitive claims and unnecessary personal details are absent.
- [ ] The record has a correction/removal route and retention decision.
- [ ] A human ethics reviewer approved publication.
- [ ] Legal or data-protection review was obtained where the risk assessment
  requires it.
